# PR-009 Maintenance, Foreign Body Control and Calibration

## 1. Purpose
This procedure ensures that buildings, equipment and measuring instruments at [SITE NAME] are maintained so they do not threaten product safety, that engineering work does not introduce contamination, and that critical measurements are accurate and reliable.

## 2. Scope
It covers planned maintenance and repairs of buildings and equipment, temporary repairs, lubricants and engineering materials, glass and hard plastic control, knives and blades, metal detectors and other foreign body detection equipment, sieves and filters, and calibration and checking of measuring instruments (thermometers, scales, loggers, pH meters, sensors).

## 3. Definitions
- **Preventive maintenance**: planned inspections and replacements reducing the risk of breakdown.
- **Temporary repair**: an interim fix used until a permanent repair, requiring risk assessment and a deadline.
- **Calibration**: comparing instrument readings with a reference of known, traceable value and determining the error.
- **Check**: internal comparison of an instrument with a working reference (e.g. reference thermometer, weights).
- **Critical instrument**: an instrument whose result is used for CCP monitoring, product release or legal compliance (e.g. net quantity).
- **Hygiene clearance**: confirmation by quality or production that after engineering work the area is clean and safe before production restarts.

## 4. Responsibilities
- Maintenance Manager: maintenance plan, carrying out work and records, control of tools and parts, supervision of contractors.
- Quality Manager: list of critical instruments, calibration plan, assessment of impact of failures on product, hygiene clearance.
- Operators: report faults, carry out daily checks (e.g. metal detector, scales), do not carry out repairs without authorisation.
- External engineers: follow PR-007 and this procedure.

## 6. Preventive maintenance
1. The Maintenance Manager keeps an equipment list with a maintenance plan (Appendix 1) based on manufacturer recommendations, breakdown history and product risk.
2. Inspections cover product risk items: loose bolts, worn seals, cracks, rust, worn belts, lubricant leaks, sieve and filter condition.
3. The plan includes the building: floors, walls, ceilings, doors, lighting (covers), ventilation and filters, water system (disinfection, tank inspection).

## 7. Work in production areas
1. Work is scheduled outside production where possible. Where urgent work is needed during production, the supervisor protects product and packaging in range.
2. Engineers record tools taken into the area (Appendix 2) and account for them afterwards. Shadow-boarded toolboxes are used.
3. Wire, bolts, cable ties, rags and parts must not be left in production. Swarf from drilling and grinding is removed immediately; grinding and welding in production only with screens and after protecting product.
4. After work: tidy, clean per IN-001 and hygiene clearance by an authorised person (entry on the work order).
5. Temporary repairs (tape, cable ties, film) are permitted only with risk assessment, labelling and a deadline for permanent repair; a register is kept.

## 8. Lubricants and engineering materials
1. Where incidental product contact is possible, food-grade lubricants and oils (e.g. NSF H1 registered or equivalent) are used, with safety data sheets.
2. Lubricants are stored in a designated place, food-grade separated from technical grades.
3. Seals, hoses, belts and other product contact parts have food contact declarations.

## 9. Foreign body control

### 9.1 Glass, brittle plastics and ceramics
1. The glass and brittle materials register (Appendix 3) covers all items in production and open-product storage areas: light covers, sight glasses, displays, clocks, windows.
2. Items are checked at a risk-based frequency (e.g. weekly in production, monthly in stores).
3. Glass is not used in production unless unavoidable and protected (e.g. polycarbonate covers, protective film).

### 9.2 Glass or hard plastic breakage
1. Stop work within [e.g. 3 m] and keep others away.
2. Hold product, packaging and raw materials in range and product made since the last good check.
3. Collect fragments with a dedicated kit (colour-coded, kept in a designated place), attempting to reconstruct the item.
4. Change clothing and footwear of those cleaning up, clean the area, quality clearance.
5. Complete the report (Appendix 4) and decide on product (usually disposal of product in range).

### 9.3 Knives and blades
Single-piece knives (no snap-off blades) are used, issued to individuals or workstations and checked at the start and end of shift. Film and tape cutters are detectable or guarded.

### 9.4 Metal detectors and X-ray
1. The detector has an automatic reject into a locked container, an alarm and a fail-safe stop if the reject fails.
2. Test pieces (e.g. Fe, non-Fe, SS at sizes from risk assessment and customer requirements) placed in the centre or least sensitive position of the pack are tested: at start-up, every [e.g. 1–2 hours], at product change, after repair and at the end of production.
3. If a test fails: product since the last good test is held and re-passed through a working detector (HA-001, CCP).
4. The reject container is locked and the key held by an authorised person; rejects are examined and recorded.

### 9.5 Sieves, filters, magnets
Sieves and magnets are checked per plan (e.g. daily) for integrity, wear and contents. Findings are recorded and assessed (source, product impact).

## 10. Calibration and checking of instruments
1. The Quality Manager keeps an instrument list (Appendix 5) with identifier, location, range, required accuracy, frequency and method of calibration or check.
2. Critical instruments are calibrated at least annually by an accredited laboratory or against a reference with traceable certification.
3. Internal checks (examples):

| Instrument | Check method | Frequency | Criterion |
|---|---|---|---|
| Probe thermometer | comparison with reference thermometer in ice bath (0°C) and boiling water or bath | [e.g. monthly] | difference ≤ [e.g. ± 0.5°C] |
| Check weigher / scale | certified test weight | [e.g. daily before use] | per scale class |
| Metal detector | test pieces | as in 9.4 | all test pieces rejected |
| Temperature loggers | comparison with reference thermometer | [e.g. quarterly] | difference ≤ [e.g. ± 0.5°C] |
| pH meter | buffer solutions | before each measurement series | per manufacturer instructions |

4. Instruments carry a label with calibration due date or status.
5. Scales used for net quantity of prepacked goods and trade are verified as required by legal metrology law.

## 11. Deviations
| Deviation | Immediate action | Corrective action | Decision by |
|---|---|---|---|
| Critical instrument out of tolerance | remove from use, label, use a spare | assess product impact since last good check, CAPA | Quality Manager |
| Metal detector failed to reject a test piece | stop line, hold product since last good test | repair, revalidate, re-inspect product | Quality Manager |
| Missing part or tool after work | stop, search, hold product | risk assessment, CAPA | Maintenance Manager and Quality Manager |
| Glass breakage | per 9.2 | replace with safe material | Quality Manager |
| Damaged sieve | hold product since last check, re-sieve | replace, change check frequency | Quality Manager |
| Lubricant leak above product | stop, hold product | repair, guards, assess lubricant type | Maintenance Manager |
| Temporary repair overdue | escalate | permanent repair | Site Director |

## 12. Verification
GMP inspections cover equipment condition, temporary repairs and the glass register. Annually the Quality Manager reviews the instrument list and calibration results (error trends) and the Maintenance Manager reviews breakdown history for product impact.
