# PR-001 Document and Record Control

## 1. Purpose
This procedure ensures that only current, approved documents are used at [SITE NAME], and that records demonstrating product safety and quality are complete, legible, truthful, secure and retained for the required time, so that the history of each batch can be reconstructed and compliance demonstrated during audits and official controls.

## 2. Scope
It covers all system documents (policies, procedures, instructions, specifications, plans, forms) and records (paper and electronic) relating to product safety, legality, authenticity and quality, as well as external documents: legislation, standards, customer and supplier specifications.

## 3. Definitions
- **Document**: information on how to do something (e.g. procedure, instruction, specification). A document is changed by issuing a new version.
- **Record**: evidence that something was done (e.g. temperature log, cleaning record). Records are not changed; errors are corrected visibly.
- **Controlled document**: a document with a number, version, date and approval, whose distribution is controlled.
- **Uncontrolled copy**: a printout or file outside the system that is not updated; it must be marked as such.
- **Data integrity (ALCOA+)**: records are attributable, legible, contemporaneous, original, accurate, and also complete, consistent, enduring and available.

## 4. Responsibilities
- Quality Manager: administers the documentation system, assigns numbers, maintains the document register, archives obsolete versions, reviews records.
- Document author: prepares drafts and changes, consults users.
- Approver (department manager or Site Director): approves documents before issue.
- Department managers: ensure current documents are available at workstations, withdraw obsolete printouts, train staff on changes.
- Employees: use only current documents and keep records in line with this procedure.
- IT Administrator: backups, access rights and audit trails in electronic systems.

- Regulation (EC) No 852/2004, Article 5(2)(g) and 5(4) (documents and records appropriate to the nature and size of the business).

## 6. Document structure and identification
1. Every document has: code, title, version number, issue date, author, approver, change history and page numbering "page X of Y".
2. Coding rules:

| Prefix | Type | Example |
|---|---|---|
| QM | top-level system document | QM-001 |
| HA | hazard analysis, HACCP plan | HA-001 |
| PR | procedure | PR-003 |
| IN | work instruction | IN-001 |
| F | form (with procedure number) | F-PR003-01 |
| SP | raw material, packaging, product specification | SP-S-012 |

3. Versions are numbered sequentially (01, 02, 03). An editorial change (typo) also raises the version but does not require training.
4. Forms are controlled documents; completed forms become records.

## 7. Drafting, approval and issue
1. The author prepares the draft on the current template, involving the people who will use it.
2. The Quality Manager checks consistency with the system, legislation, customer requirements and other documents.
3. The document is approved by the person named in the document register (Appendix 1). HACCP documents are approved by the Site Director.
4. The Quality Manager enters the document in the register, publishes it in [SYSTEM / FOLDER] and issues controlled copies to workstations according to the distribution list.
5. Before the effective date, supervisors train employees; training is recorded according to PR-007.

## 8. Change and withdrawal
1. Any employee may request a change via their supervisor or the Quality Manager.
2. Changes follow the same route as new documents. The change history states what was changed and why.
3. Where the change affects a process, product or HACCP, change management (QM-001, section 10) is applied first.
4. After a new version is issued, department managers collect old printouts within [e.g. 1 working day] and return them to the Quality Manager. Obsolete versions are archived marked "OBSOLETE" and must not return to workstations.
5. A document withdrawn without replacement is marked as withdrawn in the register with date and reason.

## 9. Periodic review
1. Each document is reviewed at least annually or at the frequency in the register, and after changes to legislation, standards, customer requirements, incidents, complaints or audit non-conformities.
2. The review outcome (no change / change) is recorded in the register with date and signature.

## 10. External documents
1. The Quality Manager maintains a register of external documents (Appendix 2): legislation, standards, guidance, customer specifications.
2. Legislative changes are monitored at least [e.g. monthly] via: EUR-Lex, national official journals, the competent authority, customer and trade association communications, RASFF.
3. The impact of each change is assessed and recorded; required actions go through change management.

## 11. Record-keeping rules
1. Records are completed at the time of the activity, in permanent ink (no pencil, no correction fluid).
2. Each entry has a date, time (where relevant), result and legible signature or initials of the person performing it. Specimen initials are kept in the signature register (Appendix 3).
3. Errors are corrected by a single strike-through so that the original remains legible, writing the correct value, date, initials and a brief reason.
4. Blank fields are filled with a dash or "n/a". Data must not be filled in "from memory" afterwards; a missing entry is a non-conformity.
5. A result outside the limit requires a description of the corrective action on the same record or a reference to a non-conformity report.
6. The supervisor verifies critical records (CCPs, product release) at the frequency in the HACCP plan and confirms this by signature.

## 12. Electronic records
1. System access is personal and password-protected; accounts are not shared.
2. The system records who created or changed data and when (audit trail), or changes are locked after approval.
3. Backups are made [FREQUENCY, e.g. daily], stored away from the primary server, and restoration is tested at least annually.
4. Data from loggers (e.g. temperature) are exported and archived before device memory is overwritten.

## 13. Storage and archiving
1. Records are stored to protect them from damage, loss and unauthorised access.
2. Minimum retention: product shelf life + 12 months, unless legislation or the customer requires longer. For products without a best-before date, [e.g. 3 years] applies.
3. Records must be retrievable within [e.g. 2 hours] for withdrawals, inspections or audits.
4. After the retention period, records are destroyed so they cannot be read; destruction is recorded in a log.

## 14. Deviations
| Deviation | Immediate action | Corrective action | Decision by |
|---|---|---|---|
| Obsolete document version at a workstation | withdraw printout, issue current version, check other workstations | root cause analysis, reminder of distribution rules | Quality Manager |
| Missing entry in a CCP record | hold batch, establish facts (loggers, witnesses) | product assessment, training, CAPA per PR-003 | Quality Manager |
| Record completed in advance or "from memory" | invalidate entry, assess impact on product | discussion with employee, training, review of food safety culture | Department manager and Quality Manager |
| Loss of electronic data | restore from backup, assess missing scope | backup review | IT Administrator |
| Correction fluid or obliteration in a record | document correction per 11.3 | training | Supervisor |

## 15. Verification
1. During internal audits (PR-006), at least [e.g. 5] documents at workstations are checked for currency and records from the last [e.g. 4 weeks] for completeness and correctness.
2. At least annually, retrieval of the full record set for one batch is tested as part of the traceability test (PR-004).
