# VIP e-book: Quality Manager Playbook — expert base

> Advanced playbook for a Quality Manager dealing with site operations, customers, audits, equipment, new technology and crisis decisions.

## How to use
Use this playbook by problem type. The objective is senior Quality Manager thinking: rapid risk assessment, product containment, communication, evidence and permanent system improvement.

## Decision map
```text
SIGNAL / PROBLEM
      │
      ├─► Food safety risk? → hold / escalation / traceability
      ├─► Legal or label risk? → requirement and customer verification
      ├─► Quality risk? → data, lot and process analysis
      └─► CAPA + effectiveness + system learning
```

## 1. Role of a senior Quality Manager
A senior Quality Manager is not a document owner only. The role owns food-safety logic and must understand production, technology, maintenance, laboratory, customer expectations, legislation, audits and operational pressure.

Core questions:
- Is the product safe?
- Is it legal for the destination market?
- Do we have evidence?
- Is the decision proportionate to risk?
- Will the system be stronger after this case?

## 2. Quality problem analysis
First 15 minutes:
1. Contain the risk: lot hold, line stop or material segregation.
2. Secure evidence: samples, photos, records, status, process parameters.
3. Define scope: lot, date, shift, line, supplier, customer, warehouse.
4. Assess safety, legality and quality.
5. Assign the investigation owner.

Use the five-layer model:
```text
SYMPTOM → LOT → PROCESS → SYSTEM → CULTURE
```

## 3. Customer complaint
A strong response confirms receipt, states containment, requests evidence, gives a response date and avoids admitting fault without data.

Minimum data: product, lot/date/code, quantity, photos, description, storage conditions, detection point and sample availability.

Report structure: complaint data, risk assessment, traceability, record review, root cause, immediate action, corrective action, effectiveness and customer response.

## Complaint path
```text
COMPLAINT → REGISTER → RISK → EVIDENCE → RCA → CAPA → RESPONSE → TREND
```

## 4. Customer communication
Use factual language: records reviewed, affected product held, food-safety impact not confirmed based on evidence, action implemented and effectiveness scheduled.

## 5. Audits before contract signature
Pre-contract audits are credibility audits. Customers assess system maturity, people, evidence, hygiene and reaction to questions.

Preparation scope: site map and flows, HACCP/PRPs, suppliers, specifications, allergens, traceability, lab plan, complaints, CAPA, hygiene, infrastructure and recent records.

## 6. New-customer implementation
Define customer specification, reporting format, test methods, tolerances, label/packaging rules, complaint path, KPI review and bilateral responsibilities before launch.

## 7. Equipment and machinery assessment
Equipment can create risk through fragments, hygienic dead zones, leaks, lubricants, condensate, dosing errors, breakdowns and poor cleanability.

| Criterion | Question | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Hygiene | Can it be cleaned and verified? | instruction, validation, swabs |
| Foreign bodies | What can detach or wear? | parts list, inspections |
| Process | Can it hold the parameter? | monitoring, calibration |
| Maintenance | What happens after repair? | post-maintenance release |

## 8. New production line
A line is not ready because it runs mechanically. It must be quality-validated: engineering readiness, hygiene approval, process trial, HACCP impact, parameter confirmation, operator training and enhanced monitoring for first lots.

## 9. Technology change
Every significant change requires assessment of product, safety, allergens, label, process parameters, cleaning, testing, customer specification and traceability.

## 10. Practical examples
Wrong label: hold product, check line and packaging stores, reconcile labels, assess allergen/legal risk, decide rework/withdrawal and improve line clearance.

Off-odour complaint: do not defend first. Check lot, transport, packaging, temperature, storage, raw material, testing, shift and trend history.

New retail customer: do not sign launch without specification, artwork requirements, testing plan, trial production and complaint pathway.

## VIP checklists
Pre-contract: requirements captured, gap analysis complete, sample/spec approved, reporting format agreed, responsibilities documented.

New line: equipment risk analysis, cleaning validation, HACCP impact, training, start-up tests, first-lot monitoring.

After issue: product contained, root cause evidenced, CAPA owned and dated, effectiveness checked, system lesson implemented.
