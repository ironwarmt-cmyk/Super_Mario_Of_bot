# PR-011 Complaints and Trend Analysis

## 1. Purpose
This procedure sets out how [SITE NAME] receives, records, investigates and closes complaints from customers and consumers, communicates with the complainant and analyses trends, so that hazards are recognised quickly, causes removed and complaint levels reduced.

## 2. Scope
It covers complaints about safety, legality, authenticity and quality of products, packaging, labels, deliveries and documents, from trade customers, consumers, official authorities and social media. Purely commercial disputes (price, payment) are excluded unless they arise from product non-conformity.

## 3. Definitions
- **Complaint**: any expression of dissatisfaction with a product or service, including reports of illness or injury.
- **Justified complaint**: one where investigation confirms a cause attributable to the site or its suppliers.
- **Complaint sample**: product or foreign body provided by the complainant.
- **Complaint rate**: number of justified complaints relative to sales volume (e.g. per million units).

## 4. Responsibilities
- Customer service / sales: receives and records complaints, passes them to quality the same day, handles correspondence.
- Quality Manager: classifies, investigates, decides on escalation, approves the response, analyses trends.
- Area managers: take part in root cause analysis and implement actions.
- Site Director: decisions on critical complaints, initiation of PR-013.

## 5. References
- Regulation (EC) No 178/2002, Article 19.
- [NATIONAL CONSUMER PROTECTION LAW] (deadlines for responding to consumers).
- Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (GDPR) for complainants' personal data.
- BRCGS Food Safety Issue 9, clause 3.10 (complaint handling); IFS Food version 8, clause 5.8.
- PR-003, PR-004, PR-010, PR-013.

## 6. Receipt and recording
1. Complaints may arrive by phone, e-mail, website form, from trade customers, from authorities or via social media.
2. The receiver collects data (Appendix 1): complainant and contact, product, weight, batch, shelf-life date, place of purchase, description, whether anyone became ill or was injured, whether a sample is available, photos.
3. The complaint is entered in the register (Appendix 2) with number COM-[YEAR]-[NO] on the day of receipt.
4. Receipt is acknowledged within [e.g. 1 working day]; the complainant is asked to keep the product and packaging.
5. Personal data are processed only to handle the complaint, in line with GDPR.

## 7. Classification
| Class | Examples | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Critical | illness or injury, hard or sharp foreign body (glass, metal), undeclared allergen, suspected poisoning, product tampering, authority control | immediate notification of Quality Manager and Site Director, assessment of hold and PR-013 within [e.g. 4 hours] |
| Major | soft foreign body (hair, soft plastic), mould before date, label error without health impact, seal failure | investigation within [e.g. 5 working days] |
| Minor | appearance, shape, taste within safety limits, underfill within tolerance, outer case damage | investigation within [e.g. 10 working days] |

## 8. Investigation
1. The Quality Manager checks:
   - batch records: production, CCPs, cleaning, metal detector, sieves, release, temperatures,
   - retained samples of the batch (if kept),
   - other complaints for this batch and type,
   - the complaint sample (foreign body identification, laboratory testing, comparison with materials on site).
2. The sample is stored securely, labelled, in conditions that preserve evidence; chain of custody is recorded.
3. Where the cause may lie with a supplier, the complaint is passed on per PR-002.
4. The cause is identified using PR-003 methods. The complaint is justified where evidence points to the site or supply chain.

## 9. Response and closure
1. The response includes: investigation results, cause (if identified), actions, apology or compensation per site policy.
2. Deadlines: trade customer per contract or [e.g. 10 working days]; consumers within the timeframe required by national law.
3. Where investigation takes longer, the complainant is updated on progress.
4. The Quality Manager closes the complaint after the response is sent and a CAPA opened (where justified).

## 10. Trend analysis
1. Monthly, the Quality Manager analyses the register: number, class, category (foreign body, microbiology, label, packaging, sensory, weight), product, line, shift, supplier.
2. The complaint rate per million units is calculated and compared with the objective (QM-001).
3. An increase in a category or a recurring issue ([e.g. 3 times per quarter]) triggers CAPA even for minor complaints.
4. Results are presented at the quality meeting and management review; key information is shared with staff (e.g. quality board).

## 11. Deviations
| Deviation | Action | Decision by |
|---|---|---|
| Critical complaint not passed on the same day | immediate escalation, assess impact of delay | Quality Manager |
| No batch number given | establish from shelf-life date and place of purchase, review all possible batches | Quality Manager |
| Response deadline missed | inform complainant, prioritise closure | Quality Manager |
| Complaint sample lost | record, explain, change storage arrangements | Quality Manager |
| Several similar complaints from one batch | hold stock, assess PR-013 | Site Director |

## 12. Verification
The internal audit samples closed complaints (investigation completeness, timeliness, CAPA). The complaint rate is a quality objective.
