# PR-005 Allergen Management

## 1. Purpose
This procedure sets out how [SITE NAME] identifies, assesses and controls allergens at every stage: from purchasing raw materials, through storage, production and cleaning, to labelling and product release. The aim is that a consumer with an allergy or intolerance receives a product whose label truthfully declares the allergens present, and that unintended presence of allergens is limited to the level justified by a documented risk assessment.

## 2. Scope
It covers all raw materials, intermediates, processing aids (e.g. food-grade lubricants, release agents, toppings), rework, finished products, printed packaging and all production, storage and laboratory lines and rooms. It also covers samples, prototypes from product development and products from subcontractors.

## 3. Definitions
- **Allergen**: a substance or product listed in Annex II to Regulation (EU) No 1169/2011 (14 groups) and derived products, plus any additional allergens required in the destination country or by the customer.
- **Intentionally added allergen**: an allergen that is a recipe ingredient and listed in the ingredients list.
- **Cross-contact**: unintended transfer of an allergen into a product that does not contain it in the recipe.
- **PAL (Precautionary Allergen Labelling)**: voluntary warning labelling, e.g. "may contain hazelnuts", used only on the basis of a risk assessment.
- **Allergen matrix**: a table showing allergen presence for each raw material, line and product.
- **Cleaning validation**: documented evidence that a cleaning method removes the allergen to an undetectable or agreed level.
- **Cleaning verification**: ongoing confirmation that the validated method was carried out correctly (visual check, swabs).

## 4. Responsibilities
- Site Director: provides resources (separate tools, cleaning time, testing) and approves decisions to introduce a new allergen to site.
- Quality Manager: procedure owner; maintains the allergen matrix and risk assessment, approves cleaning validation, decides on PAL, delivers training.
- Production Manager: plans production order, supervises changeovers and cleaning, enforces segregation on the shop floor.
- Purchasing: obtains current specifications and allergen declarations from suppliers, reports any change of material or supplier before the first delivery.
- Warehouse: receives, labels and stores allergenic materials in line with segregation rules.
- Product development: assesses the impact of new recipes on allergens before production trials.
- Operators and supervisors: clean according to instructions, check the line before start-up, report any deviation.

## 7. Procedure

### 7.1 Allergen information from suppliers
1. Before approving a raw material, purchasing obtains a specification with full composition and an allergen declaration covering intentionally added allergens and cross-contact risk at the supplier's site.
2. The Quality Manager verifies the declaration and enters the material in the allergen matrix (Appendix 1).
3. Any change of recipe, supplier, supplier production site or packer requires a new declaration before the first delivery.
4. Declarations are reviewed at least annually and on every RASFF alert or notice of change from the supplier.

### 7.2 Allergen matrix and risk assessment
1. The matrix covers all raw materials, intermediates, rework and finished products and the lines on which they are processed.
2. A cross-contact risk assessment is carried out for each line and allergen (Appendix 2), considering:
   - physical form of the allergen (dust, liquid, pieces, paste) and its ability to spread,
   - quantity in the recipe and volume produced,
   - line design (dead zones, hard-to-clean areas),
   - shared equipment, tools, containers and ventilation,
   - movement of personnel and materials,
   - effectiveness of validated cleaning,
   - consumer sensitivity (e.g. products for children, "gluten-free" products).
3. The result determines the control method: segregation, time separation with validated cleaning, or justified use of PAL.
4. The assessment is updated on any change of recipe, raw material, line, equipment or cleaning process, after any allergen incident and at least annually.

### 7.3 Receipt and storage
1. Allergenic materials are labelled at receipt with a colour assigned to the allergen (e.g. red: nuts, yellow: milk). The colour legend is displayed in the warehouse.
2. Allergenic materials are stored in designated zones, on the lowest rack levels, below non-allergenic materials.
3. Damaged packs containing allergens are isolated immediately and spills cleaned with an allergen spill kit (dedicated dustpans, filtered vacuum, bags).
4. Open allergenic packs are tightly closed and marked with the opening date.

### 7.4 Production and segregation
1. The Production Manager plans the sequence: allergen-free products first, then products with increasing numbers of allergens. The production plan shows the allergens in each batch.
2. Tools, containers, scoops and workwear for allergens have the same colour as the allergen label and are not used outside their zone.
3. Weighing of allergens takes place in a separate area or at the end of the shift, with dust extraction.
4. Rework containing an allergen may only go into a product containing the same allergen ("like into like"). Every use of rework is recorded.
5. Staff wash their hands and change outer clothing after working with an allergen before entering an allergen-free area.
6. Visitors and employees do not bring their own food onto the production floor.

### 7.5 Cleaning, validation and verification
1. Cleaning after production with an allergen follows IN-001 with additional allergen steps (dismantling, cleaning of hard-to-reach areas).
2. **Validation**: for each line and highest-risk allergen, at least three consecutive cleans are carried out with swab testing (lateral flow or ELISA) of the hardest-to-clean points and testing of the first product after cleaning. Cleaning is validated when all results are below the method's limit of detection or the agreed threshold.
3. Validation is repeated after changes to equipment, cleaning chemical, cleaning method, a recipe with a new allergen, and at least every 3 years.
4. **Ongoing verification**: after every allergen clean, the supervisor carries out a visual check against the line's critical points list (Appendix 3). The line may not be started without the releasing person's signature.
5. Periodic verification: allergen swabs at the frequency set in the risk assessment, not less than [FREQUENCY, e.g. monthly] for each line with an allergen.
6. ATP tests confirm general cleanliness but do not replace allergen tests.

### 7.6 Labelling
1. Intentionally added allergens are emphasised in the ingredients list so they stand out from other ingredients (e.g. bold), in accordance with Article 21 of Regulation (EU) No 1169/2011.
2. The label design is approved by the Quality Manager on the basis of the current recipe and allergen matrix.
3. PAL is used only where the risk assessment shows a real cross-contact risk that cannot be eliminated. PAL must not be used instead of good practice.
4. Before each batch starts, the operator checks that the label matches the product (code, version, allergens) and records this on the start-up check sheet.
5. For non-prepacked food, allergen information is available to the consumer in accordance with Article 44 and national rules.

### 7.7 Product development and changes
1. Introducing a new allergen to site requires Site Director approval following a risk assessment covering all lines.
2. Production trials with an allergen are scheduled at the end of the shift, with a full clean afterwards.
3. Any change of supplier, recipe or packaging goes through change management with an allergen impact assessment.

### 7.8 Training
1. All production, warehouse, cleaning and laboratory staff receive allergen training on induction and at least annually.
2. Training covers: the allergen list, colours and zones, production sequence, rework rules, spill handling, label checks and the consequences of errors for consumers.
3. Training effectiveness is verified by testing and observation at the workstation.

## 8. Deviations
| Situation | Immediate action | Decision by |
|---|---|---|
| Allergen spill | isolate area, clean with allergen kit, visual check, assess nearby products | supervisor, Quality Manager |
| Failed cleaning verification (positive swab) | stop start-up, re-clean, re-swab, hold product made | Quality Manager |
| Wrong label on the line | stop the line, isolate labelled packs since last good check | Production Manager, Quality Manager |
| Raw material without current declaration | hold material in the system until resolved | Quality Manager |
| Undeclared allergen in product on the market | initiate PR-013 Incident management, assess withdrawal or recall, notify authorities | crisis team |

Every deviation is recorded as a non-conformity and resolved according to PR-003 CAPA.

## 9. System verification
- Review of matrix and risk assessment: at least annually and on change.
- Internal audit of allergen management: at least annually (PR-006).
- Trend analysis of swab results, complaints and allergen incidents at management review.
- Check of a randomly selected label against the recipe: [FREQUENCY].
