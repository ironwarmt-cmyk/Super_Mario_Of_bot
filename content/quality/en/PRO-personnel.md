# PR-007 Personnel: Training, Personal Hygiene and Protective Clothing

## 1. Purpose
This procedure ensures that everyone working at [SITE NAME] or entering production areas is competent for their work, knows the hygiene rules and is not a source of product contamination, and that protective clothing protects product from contamination.

## 2. Scope
It applies to permanent and temporary employees, trainees, agency workers, contractors, service engineers, drivers entering the site, visitors and auditors. It covers health requirements, induction and refresher training, competence assessment, personal hygiene rules, protective clothing, laundry and welfare facilities.

## 3. Definitions
- **Competence**: the ability to apply knowledge and skills, confirmed by assessment rather than attendance alone.
- **Hygiene zones**: areas with different product contamination risk (e.g. low risk, high care, high risk under BRCGS) with different personnel requirements.
- **Hygiene barrier**: entry point to production where footwear and clothing are changed and hands washed and sanitised.
- **Protective clothing**: workwear intended for production that protects product from contamination.

## 4. Responsibilities
- Site Director: provides resources for training, clothing and welfare facilities.
- HR: keeps training and medical records, organises health checks, plans refresher training.
- Quality Manager: prepares hygiene, HACCP and allergen training, assesses training effectiveness, monitors compliance.
- Supervisors (shift managers, team leaders): train on the job, assess competence, enforce hygiene rules daily.
- Employees: follow the rules and report illness symptoms before starting work.

## 5. References
- Regulation (EC) No 852/2004, Annex II, Chapter VIII (personal hygiene) and Chapter XII (training).
- [NATIONAL REQUIREMENTS FOR MEDICAL FITNESS OF FOOD HANDLERS].
- BRCGS Food Safety Issue 9, section 7 (personnel); IFS Food version 8, chapter 3 (resource management).
- PR-005, IN-001, PR-014.

## 6. Health requirements
1. Before working with food, the employee meets the medical fitness requirements of national law (e.g. a food handler health certificate where required).
2. Before entering production, the employee reports to the supervisor: diarrhoea, vomiting, fever, jaundice, skin infections, infected wounds, discharge from eyes, ears or nose, contact with someone suffering from a foodborne disease, and illness while travelling.
3. The supervisor decides on exclusion from food handling. Return after a gastrointestinal infection requires medical clearance; after diarrhoea or vomiting the employee returns to food handling only once symptoms have stopped, for the period set in the site risk assessment or by a doctor (e.g. 48 hours symptom-free).
4. Visitors and contractors complete a health questionnaire (Appendix 4) before entering production.
5. Minor cuts are covered with a detectable (metallised), coloured plaster and, where needed, a disposable glove. Plaster issue is recorded and checked at the end of the shift.

## 7. Personal hygiene rules
1. Hands are washed and sanitised: before entering production, after breaks, after using the toilet, after handling waste, raw materials or outer packaging, after coughing, sneezing or touching the face, and when changing tasks.
2. Hand washing method (instructions at basins): wet, soap, wash for at least [e.g. 20–30 s] including between fingers and thumbs, rinse, dry with a disposable towel, sanitise.
3. Prohibited in production and open-product storage areas: jewellery (only a plain wedding band where the risk assessment permits), watches, visible earrings and piercings, false nails, nail varnish, eyelash extensions, strongly scented products.
4. Prohibited: eating, drinking (except at designated points with water in closed containers where the risk assessment permits), chewing gum, smoking, bringing medicines and personal items. Personal phones stay in the locker room unless the risk assessment allows otherwise.
5. Nails short and clean. Hair fully covered by a hairnet or cap; beards covered with a beard snood.
6. Essential medication (e.g. inhaler) is kept in a designated place agreed with the supervisor.

## 8. Protective clothing
1. Clothing is provided by the site. It is light-coloured or colour-coded by zone, without external pockets above the waist and without buttons (concealed press studs).
2. Clothing is put on at the hygiene barrier in the order: hair covering (and beard snood), workwear, footwear via the transfer area (bench or disinfection mat per site layout), hand washing and sanitising.
3. Protective clothing is not worn outside the site, in toilets or smoking areas. Aprons and coats are removed in a designated place before using the toilet.
4. Clothing is laundered by a laundry approved as a service provider (PR-002) or on site, using a process that ensures cleanliness (temperature and chemicals per risk assessment). Employees do not launder workwear at home unless the risk assessment for low-risk areas permits it with written rules.
5. Change frequency: [e.g. daily in high-risk areas, at least twice weekly elsewhere] and whenever soiled.
6. Disposable gloves, where used, are in a colour contrasting with the product (e.g. blue) and changed as for hand washing; they do not replace hand washing.

## 9. Training

### 9.1 Induction (before starting work)
| Topic | Who | Duration (example) | Assessment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Personal hygiene and illness reporting | everyone | 1 h | test, observation at hygiene barrier |
| Basic HACCP and CCPs at the workstation | production, warehouse, quality | 1 h | test |
| Allergens on site (PR-005) | all production staff | 0.5 h | test |
| Foreign bodies, glass, hard plastics, knives | production | 0.5 h | discussion |
| Food safety culture, how to report concerns | everyone | 0.5 h | discussion |
| Food defence (PR-014) | everyone | 0.25 h | discussion |
| Workstation instruction | individual | per workstation | observation by supervisor |

Training is delivered in a language the employee understands, with an interpreter or translated materials and pictograms where needed.

### 9.2 Refresher and specialist training
1. Hygiene, HACCP and allergen refresher at least annually for everyone.
2. CCP operators: monitoring and corrective action training before working unsupervised and annually.
3. HACCP team, internal auditors, product release staff: specialist training with documented certificates.
4. Additional training after procedure changes, new products, incidents or recurring non-conformities.

### 9.3 Skills matrix
Supervisors keep a skills matrix (Appendix 1) showing who is trained and assessed for each workstation and who can cover absences. Only people marked competent in the matrix may work at a CCP.

### 9.4 Effectiveness
Training effectiveness is assessed by test (pass mark [e.g. 80%]), observation at the workstation within [e.g. 1 month] and KPIs (GMP inspection results, non-conformities). Unsatisfactory results mean repeat training.

## 10. Visitors, service engineers and contractors
1. Before entry: sign-in, health questionnaire, brief hygiene rules (visitor card), protective clothing, escort by site staff if not trained.
2. Service engineers declare tools and parts taken into production and account for them on completion (PR-009).

## 11. Welfare facilities
1. Locker rooms separating personal and protective clothing; toilets not opening directly onto production; hand basins with non-hand-operated taps, soap, disposable towels and sanitiser.
2. Eating only in the canteen or designated area; personal food in a labelled fridge outside production.
3. Welfare facilities are in the cleaning schedule (IN-001).

## 12. Compliance monitoring
1. Supervisors check at shift start: clothing, jewellery, plasters, health (question), hand washing.
2. The Quality Manager performs a personal hygiene check at least [e.g. monthly], including hand swabs (e.g. total viable count, Enterobacteriaceae or coagulase-positive staphylococci per testing plan PR-017).

## 13. Deviations
| Deviation | Immediate action | Corrective action | Decision by |
|---|---|---|---|
| Employee with gastrointestinal symptoms | exclude from food handling, assess product handled | interview, testing if needed, reminder of reporting rules | Supervisor, Quality Manager |
| Jewellery or watch in production | remove, check completeness (e.g. stones in ring) | discussion, training | Supervisor |
| Lost plaster | stop line, search, assess product (metal detector) | root cause | Supervisor, Quality Manager |
| No hand washing after break | wash hands immediately | training, observations | Supervisor |
| Employee without valid medical fitness | exclude from food handling | complete, review HR records | HR |
| Hand swab out of criterion | re-wash, training | re-swab, check soap and towels | Quality Manager |
| Non-competent employee at a CCP | replace with competent person, review records | update matrix, training | Production Manager |
