# PR-012 Labelling, Packaging Control and Product Release

## 1. Purpose
This procedure ensures that every [SITE NAME] product reaches the market in the correct packaging, with a correct, legally compliant label matching the recipe, a legible batch code and date, and is released for sale only after all required checks have been confirmed.

## 2. Scope
It covers: drafting and approving label artwork, ordering and receiving printed packaging, packaging and coding checks on the line, packaging changeovers, handling of unused and obsolete packaging, and release of finished products.

## 3. Definitions
- **Food information**: all mandatory and voluntary information provided to the consumer (Regulation (EU) No 1169/2011).
- **Artwork**: graphic design of the packaging before printing.
- **Line packaging check**: confirmation that the correct packaging and label are on the line and that batch and date coding is correct and legible.
- **Positive release**: product is released only after required test results (e.g. microbiological) are available.

## 4. Responsibilities
- Technologist / development: prepares label text from the recipe and specification.
- Quality Manager: verifies legal and recipe compliance, approves artwork, releases products.
- Customer (private label): approves artwork within the scope set in the contract; the site remains responsible for the text matching the recipe.
- Purchasing Manager: orders packaging only to approved artwork, withdraws old stock.
- Packing operators: check packaging and coding at start-up, during the run and at changeover.
- Warehouse: issues packaging per plan, stores it separated by version.

## 6. Label content – mandatory list
Each artwork is checked for completeness against the checklist (Appendix 1):
1. Name of the food (legal, customary or descriptive).
2. List of ingredients in descending order of weight, with allergens emphasised (e.g. bold).
3. Allergens per Annex II (14 groups), including in compound ingredients and processing aids.
4. Quantity of certain ingredients (QUID) where the ingredient appears in the name or is emphasised by words or images.
5. Net quantity.
6. Best-before date ("best before…") or use-by date ("use by…") for microbiologically highly perishable foods.
7. Storage conditions and conditions of use, including after opening where needed.
8. Name and address of the responsible food business operator.
9. Country or place of origin where required.
10. Instructions for use where needed.
11. Nutrition declaration (energy, fat, saturates, carbohydrate, sugars, protein, salt) unless exempt.
12. Batch code.
13. Specific additional requirements: identification mark (food of animal origin), "packaged in a protective atmosphere", sweetener or caffeine statements, health claims per the EU register.
14. Legibility: x-height of at least 1.2 mm (0.9 mm for packages whose largest surface is under 80 cm²).
15. Language required in the country of sale.

## 7. Artwork approval
1. The technologist prepares the text from the current recipe, raw material specifications (including allergens and origin) and test results (e.g. nutrition calculated or tested, shelf life confirmed by testing).
2. The Quality Manager compares the artwork with the recipe and checklist; two independent people check the artwork ("four eyes" principle).
3. Artwork is approved by signature with date and version number (Appendix 2). For private label, the customer's approval is attached.
4. The first print run (proof or first delivery) is compared with the approved artwork before issue to production.
5. Any change of recipe, raw material, supplier (allergens, origin) or legislation triggers a label review (change management, QM-001).

## 8. Packaging in storage
1. Packaging is stored in a clean store, in outer cases, labelled with product and version.
2. When a version changes, old packaging is held and destroyed, or used up only if the Quality Manager permits in writing (never for changes to allergens or other safety-relevant information).
3. Unused packaging returns from the line to the store closed and labelled; loose labels are not left on the line after an order.

## 9. Line checks
1. **Start-up and product changeover**: before packing starts, the operator removes all packaging and labels of the previous product from the line, and a supervisor or quality confirms on the line release sheet (Appendix 3) that:
   - the correct packaging and label are on the line (compared with a master sample or barcode),
   - batch, date and price (where applicable) coding is correct and legible,
   - metal detector and check weigher are set for the correct product.
2. **During the run**: coding and packaging checked every [e.g. 30 minutes] and at every new film reel or label batch. Barcode scanners or vision systems with automatic reject are used where possible.
3. **End**: reconcile packaging used and returned, remove remnants, record.
4. Net quantity is controlled per prepacked goods legislation (average and tolerable negative errors), using verified scales.

## 10. Finished product release
1. Product is released by the Quality Manager or an authorised person after checking (Appendix 4):
   - completeness and compliance of CCP and PRP records,
   - line release and packaging checks,
   - results of tests required for release (positive release where required by risk assessment or customer),
   - no open holds on the batch.
2. Until release, product has "awaiting release" status in the system and may not be dispatched.
3. Release is recorded in the system and on the batch record with date and signature.

## 11. Deviations
| Deviation | Immediate action | Corrective action | Decision by |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wrong packaging or label on the line | stop packing, hold product since last good check, repack | root cause (changeover, warehouse), scanners, CAPA | Quality Manager |
| Allergen missing from label | hold all batches, assess PR-013 (usually withdrawal or recall) | review artwork approval process, critical CAPA | Site Director |
| Wrong or illegible date / batch code | stop, hold, re-code or repack | printer review, more frequent checks | Quality Manager |
| Packaging delivery differs from artwork | hold delivery | complaint to supplier | Quality Manager |
| Product dispatched without release | assess records, possibly hold at customer | system blocks, CAPA | Quality Manager |
| Average net quantity below declared | stop, reweigh batch | correct filling, calibration | Production Manager |

## 12. Verification
1. At least annually, all labels on sale are reviewed against current recipes and legislation (Appendix 1).
2. The internal audit covers artwork approval and line checks (observation of a changeover).
