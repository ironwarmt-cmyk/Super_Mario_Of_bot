## 1. Food safety logic
Food safety means controlling what can realistically harm consumers or make the product illegal. Hazard analysis must be connected to product, process, raw materials, packaging, consumers and target market.

## 2. Hazard categories
Biological hazards include pathogens, growth, toxins, viruses and parasites. Chemical hazards include residues, contaminants, migration, incorrect additive dosing and cleaning residues. Physical hazards include metal, glass, hard plastic, wood, stones and equipment fragments. Allergen hazards include cross-contact, undeclared presence, wrong packaging, uncontrolled rework and recipe changes. Authenticity hazards include substitution, dilution and misleading origin. Defence hazards include deliberate contamination or unauthorised access.

## 3. Significance assessment
Do not rely only on a score. For every hazard record the source, likelihood, severity, current controls, control evidence and whether the control is sufficient.

## Decision tree
```text
Significant hazard?
       ├─ NO → PRP / general control
       └─ YES
           ├─ Effective control exists? → NO: redesign the process
           └─ Loss of control creates unacceptable risk with no later elimination?
                    ├─ YES → consider critical control
                    └─ NO → operational control + verification
```

## 4. PRPs/GHPs vs HACCP
PRPs/GHPs are the foundation: hygiene, cleaning, pest control, suppliers, infrastructure, training, waste and storage. HACCP does not repair weak PRPs.

## 5. Allergens and labels
Common real incident sources are wrong packaging, residue from previous production, uncontrolled rework, recipe changes without artwork control and suppliers changing ingredient composition.

## 6. Authenticity
A robust vulnerability assessment considers fraud history, price pressure, availability, supply-chain complexity, detectability and customer-facing claims.

## 7. Food defence
A product-protection plan should identify sensitive points such as chemicals, water, open lines, packaging, visitors and contractors.

## 8. Validation and verification
Validation asks whether a control should work. Verification asks whether it is working. Use trials, scientific data, test results, record review, audits, trends and complaints.

## Practical case
Foreign body complaint: contain affected lots, review detection, start-up records and maintenance, inspect tools and equipment, reconcile exposed product, establish root cause and verify the permanent action.
