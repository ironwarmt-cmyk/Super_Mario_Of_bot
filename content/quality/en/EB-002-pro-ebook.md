For quality managers, technologists and HACCP team leaders who know the basics and want to conduct hazard analysis at the level expected by BRCGS and IFS auditors and by official inspectors. It covers biological, chemical and physical hazards, allergens, authenticity and food defence, validation and verification, environmental monitoring and a complete worked example.

## 1. Food safety logic
Food safety is a chain: **hazard → cause → control measure → criterion → monitoring → reaction → evidence**. If one link is missing, the system has a hole.

| Link | Question | Typical gap |
|---|---|---|
| Hazard | What exactly could cause harm? | vague wording: "microbiological contamination" instead of "survival of Salmonella due to insufficient heating" |
| Cause | Where does it come from? | no cause – no way to choose a control |
| Control measure | What prevents, eliminates or reduces it? | a measure that does not act on that hazard |
| Criterion | Which value is safe? | value without source or validation |
| Monitoring | How quickly will loss of control be detected? | frequency too low to secure product |
| Reaction | What about the process and the product? | only "fixed", no product decision |
| Evidence | What will we show the auditor? | no record or unreliable record |

## 2. Biological hazards

### 2.1 Key pathogens
| Organism | Typical sources | Control-relevant features | Example controls |
|---|---|---|---|
| Salmonella spp. | eggs, poultry, meat, spices, dry products (cocoa, milk powder) | survives long in dry environments; destroyed by adequate heat treatment | heat treatment, supplier control, raw/cooked separation, hygiene |
| Listeria monocytogenes | factory environment (drains, wet areas), raw meat, fish, vegetables | grows at chill temperatures; forms biofilms; dangerous for pregnant women and the elderly | heat treatment, high-risk zones, environmental monitoring, shelf-life limits, formulation (pH, aw, preservatives) |
| STEC E. coli (e.g. O157) | beef, raw milk, leafy greens, sprouts | very low infectious dose | heat treatment, suppliers, vegetable washing |
| Campylobacter | poultry | does not grow in food but spreads easily by cross-contamination | heat treatment, raw/cooked separation |
| Clostridium botulinum | vacuum or hermetically packed products, low-acid canned foods | spores; toxin; non-proteolytic strains grow at chill temperatures | canning sterilisation, pH and aw control, temperature and shelf life of vacuum-packed products |
| Clostridium perfringens | large batches of cooked meat dishes | spores survive cooking, multiply during slow cooling | rapid cooling, hot holding at high temperature |
| Bacillus cereus | rice, pasta, spices | spores, heat-stable emetic toxin | rapid cooling, limited storage time |
| Staphylococcus aureus | hands and noses of workers | heat-stable enterotoxin | personal hygiene, temperature control |
| Norovirus, hepatitis A | ill workers, soft fruit, shellfish | low infectious dose | illness reporting, hand washing, suppliers |

### 2.2 Growth factors
Microorganisms need suitable conditions. Limiting one or more controls growth:
- **temperature**: the danger zone for most pathogens is roughly 5–60°C; Listeria also grows in chill,
- **water activity (aw)**: the lower it is, the fewer organisms can grow,
- **pH**: acidic products restrict many pathogens,
- **atmosphere**: vacuum or modified atmosphere inhibits aerobes but may favour anaerobes,
- **preservatives and time**.

Combining several factors (hurdle technology) allows a safe product with milder parameters. Every such combination must be confirmed: by testing, challenge test or predictive modelling.

### 2.3 Microbiological criteria
Regulation (EC) No 2073/2005 distinguishes:
- **food safety criteria**: apply to product placed on the market; exceeding them means the batch is unsafe and must be withdrawn,
- **process hygiene criteria**: indicate whether the process works properly; exceeding them requires process improvement.

Example: for ready-to-eat foods able to support Listeria monocytogenes growth, the criterion is "not detected in 25 g" before the food leaves the producer's control, unless the producer can demonstrate it will not exceed 100 cfu/g throughout shelf life (then the 100 cfu/g limit applies on the market). Details and sampling plans must be checked in the current text for the relevant food category.

## 3. Chemical hazards
| Group | Examples | Source | Control |
|---|---|---|---|
| Natural contaminants | mycotoxins (aflatoxins, ochratoxin A, deoxynivalenol), tropane and ergot alkaloids | cereals, nuts, spices, dried fruit | supplier specifications and certificates, testing plan, storage conditions (humidity) |
| Environmental contaminants | heavy metals (lead, cadmium, mercury), dioxins | fish, offal, vegetables from contaminated land | suppliers, periodic testing |
| Process contaminants | acrylamide, 3-MCPD, PAHs | baking, frying, smoking, oil refining | process parameters (temperature, colour), raw material choice, monitoring per Reg. (EU) 2017/2158 |
| Residues | pesticides, veterinary medicines | plant and animal raw materials | suppliers, testing |
| Site chemicals | detergents, disinfectants, lubricants | cleaning, maintenance | rinsing, food-grade lubricants, separate chemical storage |
| Migration from packaging | monomers, plasticisers, printing inks | food contact materials | declarations of compliance, migration testing |
| Additives | overdosing, unauthorised additive | recipe or dosing error | recipe control, weighing, specifications |

Maximum levels for contaminants are set by Regulation (EU) 2023/915 and pesticide residues by Regulation (EC) No 396/2005.

## 4. Physical hazards
| Foreign body | Typical source | Prevention | Detection |
|---|---|---|---|
| Metal | machine parts, sieves, knives, wire, staples | maintenance, sieve and knife checks, no staples | metal detector, magnets |
| Glass and brittle plastic | lights, sight glasses, glass packaging | glass register and checks, covers | visual inspection, X-ray (glass in jars) |
| Hard plastic | containers, scoops, belts | condition checks, replacement, contrasting colours, detectable plastics | X-ray (where density allows), detector for detectable plastics |
| Wood | pallets | no wooden pallets in open-product areas | visual inspection |
| Stones, stalks | plant raw materials | specifications, cleaning, sorting | optical sorters, sieves |
| Bones | meat, fish | specifications, inspection | X-ray |
| Personal items | jewellery, plasters, pens | hygiene rules, detectable plasters | metal detector |

A metal detector does not replace prevention. It detects only part of contamination (depending on size, metal, product and packaging). Sensitivity is set for the specific product and confirmed with test pieces (Fe, non-Fe, stainless steel).

## 5. Advanced allergen management

### 5.1 Cross-contact risk assessment
For each allergen and line assess:
| Factor | Higher risk | Lower risk |
|---|---|---|
| Physical form | dust, flour, powder | liquid, whole pieces |
| Quantity | large part of recipe | trace in an additive |
| Equipment | hard to clean (screws, hoppers, filters) | smooth, accessible |
| Separation | shared line, open processes | dedicated line, closed processes |
| Cleaning | dry, not validated | wet, validated |
| Sequence | no plan | allergen at end of day or before full clean |

### 5.2 Allergen cleaning validation
1. Choose the worst case (stickiest allergen, hardest equipment).
2. Clean according to the instruction.
3. Take swabs from critical points and samples of the first product after cleaning.
4. Test with an allergen-specific method (e.g. ELISA or lateral flow) able to detect the allergen in that matrix.
5. Repeat at least three times with negative results.
6. Verify routinely (e.g. test after every allergen clean or per plan).

Remember: a general protein test shows cleanliness but does not always detect a specific allergen. ATP does not detect allergens.

### 5.3 "May contain" and "free from" claims
- Use "may contain" only on the basis of a documented risk assessment where the risk cannot be removed.
- A "gluten-free" claim requires no more than 20 mg/kg gluten in the food as sold (Implementing Regulation (EU) No 828/2014) and a system that ensures it (suppliers, separation, testing).
- "Free from [allergen]" claims require a very high level of control; customers often require dedicated lines.

## 6. Hazard significance
Example 5 × 5 matrix (likelihood × severity):

| Likelihood \ Severity | 1 no effect | 2 discomfort | 3 mild illness | 4 serious illness | 5 life-threatening |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 frequent | 5 | 10 | 15 | 20 | 25 |
| 4 likely | 4 | 8 | 12 | 16 | 20 |
| 3 possible | 3 | 6 | 9 | 12 | 15 |
| 2 unlikely | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 | 10 |
| 1 practically impossible | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |

The significance threshold (e.g. ≥ 12) is set and justified by the team. Assess likelihood as if the control measures at that step were not working – otherwise every hazard comes out "low". Use data: site history, complaints, RASFF alerts, literature.

## 7. PRP, OPRP and CCP
| Feature | PRP | OPRP | CCP |
|---|---|---|---|
| Scope | general hygiene conditions | specific significant hazard | specific significant hazard |
| Criterion | rules, frequencies | action criterion (measurable or observable) | critical limit (measurable) |
| Loss of control | improve conditions | assess product | product considered potentially unsafe, product decision mandatory |
| Example | cleaning schedule, pests, hand hygiene | allergen segregation, sieves, chiller temperature | pasteurisation, metal detection, baking |

OPRP is an ISO 22000 concept. BRCGS and IFS work mainly with CCPs and prerequisite programmes but accept the logical distinction if it is justified in the analysis.

CCP test: if control at this step fails, will any later step remove the hazard? If not – and the control is essential – it is a CCP.

## 8. Validation, monitoring, verification
| | Validation | Monitoring | Verification |
|---|---|---|---|
| Question | Can this control work? | Is it working now? | Did it work as planned? |
| When | before implementation and after change | during production | after the event, periodically |
| Example (pasteurisation) | studies showing the parameters destroy the target pathogen at the coldest point of the product | temperature and time reading for every batch | record review, thermometer calibration, product testing |

Validation sources: legislation (e.g. pasteurisation parameters in Regulation (EC) No 853/2004), scientific literature, industry guidance, equipment manufacturer data, own studies (e.g. core temperature in worst-case conditions, challenge tests).

HACCP plan verification includes at least: CCP record review, calibration, product and environmental testing, complaint analysis, internal audit and an annual review of the whole plan.

## 9. Environmental monitoring and Listeria
For ready-to-eat producers, environmental monitoring is mandatory (Article 5(2) of Regulation (EC) No 2073/2005) and key to Listeria control.

### 9.1 Zones
| Zone | Description | Example | Testing |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | contact surfaces | belts, knives, tables | Listeria spp.; positive = product assessment |
| 2 | surfaces near zone 1 | machine frames, control panels | Listeria spp. |
| 3 | other surfaces in the room | floors, drains, trolleys | Listeria spp.; early warning |
| 4 | outside production | corridors, changing rooms | as needed |

### 9.2 Good practice
- Look for Listeria, not for proof of its absence: swab during production, in hard-to-clean places, large areas with a sponge.
- A positive in zone 2–3 is a signal, not a failure: intensive cleaning, vector swabbing around the point, searching for the source (often a drain, seal or trolley).
- Repeated positives at the same point indicate a harbourage (niche) – dismantle the equipment or change its design.
- Analyse trends: a site map with 12 months of results shows transfer routes.

## 10. Shelf life
The producer sets shelf life and must justify it. Study elements:
- realistic storage conditions and "abuse" (e.g. consumer refrigeration warmer than declared),
- microbiological testing (relevant pathogens, spoilage organisms), physico-chemical and sensory testing at time points,
- safety margin (testing beyond the declared date),
- for RTE products supporting Listeria: studies per EU Reference Laboratory (EURL Lm) guidance – shelf-life studies, challenge tests, modelling.

A best-before date relates to quality. A use-by date is used for microbiologically highly perishable food that may present a danger after that date.

## 11. Authenticity and food defence
| | Food fraud (authenticity) | Food defence |
|---|---|---|
| Motive | economic gain | causing harm: revenge, ideology, extortion |
| Who | often a supplier or intermediary | employee, outsider |
| Tool | vulnerability assessment (VACCP) | threat assessment (TACCP) |
| Example | honey with syrup, coloured spices, fish species | deliberate addition of a substance to a tank |
| Control | suppliers, authenticity testing, supply chain | access, locks, seals, monitoring, culture |

Information sources on fraud: RASFF portal, monthly reports of the EU Knowledge Centre for Food Fraud and Quality, national authority communications.

## 12. Worked example: ham and cheese sandwich (RTE, chilled)

### 12.1 Product description
| Field | Entry |
|---|---|
| Composition | wheat bread with sesame, sliced cooked ham, cheese, butter, lettuce |
| Allergens | gluten (wheat), sesame, milk; possibly mustard (if sauce), soy (in ham, depending on supplier) |
| Parameters | no microbial reduction step after assembly; product supports Listeria growth |
| Packaging | tray with film, air |
| Storage | 0–4°C, [x] days |
| Consumer | general population including vulnerable groups; eaten without further treatment |

### 12.2 Extract from the hazard analysis
| Step | Hazard | Cause | L | S | Score | Control | Classification |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ham receipt | B: Listeria monocytogenes | post-process contamination at supplier | 3 | 5 | 15 | approved certified supplier, specification, CoA, temperature at receipt | PRP (suppliers) + testing plan |
| Lettuce receipt | B: STEC, Listeria, norovirus | contamination in growing | 3 | 5 | 15 | washed ready-to-eat lettuce from approved supplier or validated washing with disinfection on site | OPRP / CCP depending on process |
| Chilled storage | B: Listeria growth | temperature too high | 3 | 5 | 15 | ≤ 4°C, logger with alarm | OPRP |
| Assembly | B: Listeria from environment, S. aureus from hands | inadequate hygiene, niches in equipment | 3 | 5 | 15 | high-care zone, hygiene, environmental monitoring, limited time at ambient | PRP + environmental monitoring |
| Assembly | A: sesame in sesame-free product | shared line | 3 | 4 | 12 | production sequence, validated cleaning, label check | OPRP |
| Packing | A: wrong label | error at product change | 2 | 5 | 10 | label checks at start and every [x] min, scanner | CCP (many producers treat label checks as an allergen CCP) |
| Packing | P: metal | equipment parts | 2 | 4 | 8 | maintenance, metal detector | CCP (if detector is the last barrier) |
| Dispatch | B: pathogen growth | cold chain break | 2 | 5 | 10 | vehicle and product temperature checks | PRP |

Lesson from the example: for an RTE product without an elimination step, suppliers, environmental hygiene, temperature and shelf life matter most. There is no single "magic" CCP – safety comes from a set of controls.

### 12.3 Verification plan for the example
| Activity | Frequency |
|---|---|
| Product testing for Listeria monocytogenes (end of shelf life) | [e.g. weekly] |
| Environmental monitoring zones 1–3 | [e.g. weekly, rotating] |
| Allergen cleaning verification with sesame test | after every allergen clean |
| Review of temperature and label records | daily |
| Shelf-life review | annually and after recipe or supplier change |
