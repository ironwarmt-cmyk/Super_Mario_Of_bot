| Field | Entry |

## Learning objectives
After the training the participant:
- explains what BRCGS Food Safety is, who it applies to and how certification works,
- knows the structure of the standard and the fundamental requirements,
- understands the requirements for senior management commitment and food safety culture,
- can identify evidence of implementation for key requirements,
- knows how non-conformities and grades are classified,
- can prepare the site and team for an audit.

## Module 1. Introduction

### 1.1 What BRCGS Food Safety is
BRCGS (Brand Reputation Compliance Global Standards) Food Safety is an international standard for food manufacturers, benchmarked by the Global Food Safety Initiative (GFSI). Many retailers, particularly in the United Kingdom and export markets, require suppliers to hold BRCGS or another GFSI-benchmarked certificate.

Issue 9 was published in August 2022; audits against it have been carried out since February 2023. BRCGS publishes position statements and is preparing the next issue – before an audit, check which issue and position statements apply.

### 1.2 Structure of the standard
| Section | Scope |
|---|---|
| 1 | Senior management commitment (including food safety culture, organisational structure) |
| 2 | The food safety plan – HACCP |
| 3 | Food safety and quality management system (documents, records, internal audits, suppliers, specifications, CAPA, non-conforming product, traceability, complaints, incidents and recalls, customer communication) |
| 4 | Site standards (external standards, food defence, layout, building fabric, utilities, equipment, maintenance, staff facilities, foreign bodies, housekeeping and hygiene, waste, pests, storage, transport) |
| 5 | Product control (development, labelling, allergens, authenticity, packaging, testing, release) |
| 6 | Process control (operations, labelling and pack control, quantity, calibration) |
| 7 | Personnel (training, hygiene, medical screening, protective clothing) |
| 8 | High-risk, high-care and ambient high-care production risk zones |
| 9 | Requirements for traded products |

### 1.3 Fundamental requirements
Some requirements are designated as fundamental. A serious failure of a fundamental requirement (critical or major non-conformity against its statement of intent) usually prevents certification. In Issue 9 fundamental requirements cover, among others: senior management commitment and continual improvement, the HACCP plan, internal audits, management of suppliers of raw materials and packaging, corrective and preventive actions, traceability, layout and product flow, housekeeping and hygiene, allergen management, control of operations and training. Check the list in the current text of the standard.

### 1.4 Audit options
| Option | Description |
|---|---|
| Announced | date agreed with the site |
| Unannounced | no prior notice; grade marked with "+" (e.g. AA+); available to every site |
| Blended | document review remotely, site part on site (where the certification body and BRCGS allow) |

## Module 2. Management, culture and HACCP

### 2.1 Senior management commitment (section 1)
The auditor checks whether management really leads the system rather than just signing documents. Evidence:
| Requirement | Example evidence |
|---|---|
| Food safety and quality policy | signed, known to employees (floor interviews) |
| Objectives | measurable, with owners, discussed at meetings |
| Food safety culture plan | activities, timescales, measurement, progress review |
| Management review | at least annually, with inputs and decisions |
| Resources | budget for repairs, training, testing |
| Confidential reporting | a system employees know and use |
| Keeping up to date | monitoring changes in law, standard, industry information |
| Management presence | attendance at quality meetings, walkabouts, audit closing meeting |

### 2.2 HACCP plan (section 2)
The standard is based on Codex Alimentarius principles. The auditor focuses on:
- composition and competence of the HACCP team,
- complete flow diagrams confirmed on site,
- hazard analysis including allergens, with links to food defence and fraud requirements,
- CCP rationale and validation of critical limits,
- monitoring and corrective actions – on the floor and in records,
- verification and review of the plan at least annually and after changes.

### 2.3 Exercise 1 – culture by numbers
Propose three measurable food safety culture indicators for your site and one action for each that could improve it within 6 months.

## Module 3. Management system (section 3)

### 3.1 Key requirements and evidence
| Area | What the auditor looks at | Typical non-conformities |
|---|---|---|
| Documents and records | currency at workstations, correct corrections, completeness | obsolete printouts, correction fluid, missing signatures |
| Internal audits | risk-based programme spread over at least four dates per year, competent and independent auditors, hygiene inspections | one "big" audit per year, auditor auditing own department |
| Suppliers | risk assessment of raw materials, approval, monitoring, service providers | no risk assessment per material, expired certificates |
| Specifications | for raw materials, packaging and products, reviewed | specifications years old without review |
| CAPA | root cause analysis for non-conformities | corrections without root cause |
| Non-conforming product | labelling, isolation, decision by authorised person | held product without label, decisions without rationale |
| Traceability | test at least annually, both directions, with quantity reconciliation, within the required time (the standard expects data quickly; 4 hours is the usual target) | no reconciliation, rework and packaging not included |
| Complaints | analysis, trends, actions | no trend analysis |
| Incidents and recalls | procedure, contact list, test at least annually; certification body notified of a recall within 3 working days | out-of-date contacts, no test |

### 3.2 Exercise 2 – traceability test
Choose a product from last week. In 20 minutes, list the documents needed to establish: raw material and packaging batches, process parameters, quantities produced, dispatched and in stock, customers. Identify where the chain could "break".

## Module 4. Site standards (section 4) and zones (section 8)

### 4.1 Key areas
| Area | Key questions |
|---|---|
| External standards and security (4.1) | Is the site secured? Is it known who has access? |
| Food defence (4.2) | Is there a threat assessment and protection plan for vulnerable points? |
| Layout and flows (4.3) | Do flows of people, materials, waste and products minimise risk? Are zones defined? |
| Building fabric (4.4) | Are surfaces in good condition, cleanable, free of condensation? |
| Utilities (4.5) | Are water, steam, ice, air and gases in contact with product controlled? |
| Equipment and maintenance (4.6–4.7) | Are there planned maintenance, hygiene clearance after work and control of temporary repairs? |
| Foreign bodies (4.9–4.10) | Glass and plastics register, knives, detectors and testing, sieves, magnets |
| Housekeeping and hygiene (4.11) | Schedules, validation, verification, environmental monitoring |
| Waste, pests, storage, transport (4.12–4.16) | Are conditions monitored and recorded? |

### 4.2 Production risk zones
The standard defines risk zones by product and process: low risk, high care, high risk and ambient high care. High-care and high-risk zones require, among others: physical segregation, controlled entry with clothing and footwear change, filtered air at appropriate pressure, dedicated equipment and an environmental monitoring programme.

### 4.3 Exercise 3 – site map
Draw a simplified plan of your site and mark: flows of raw materials, product, waste and personnel; hand wash stations; allergen and chemical storage. Find two points where "dirty" and "clean" routes cross and propose a solution.

## Module 5. Product, process, personnel (sections 5–7)

### 5.1 Product control (section 5)
| Requirement | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Product development (5.1) | risk assessment at design stage, trials, shelf-life testing |
| Labelling (5.2) | legal and recipe compliance, approval process, change control |
| Allergens (5.3) | risk assessment, segregation, cleaning validation, justified precautionary labelling |
| Authenticity (5.4) | vulnerability assessment, claims supported by documents, mass balance |
| Packaging (5.5) | specifications, compliance with food contact requirements |
| Testing (5.6) | risk-based plan, ISO/IEC 17025-accredited laboratories for critical tests |
| Product release (5.7) | release procedure and authorised persons |

### 5.2 Process control (section 6)
- process specifications and protection of equipment settings,
- label and packaging checks on the line at start-up and product changeover,
- quantity control (net weight),
- calibration and checking of critical instruments with identification.

### 5.3 Personnel (section 7)
- training before starting work and competence assessment, including agency staff,
- personal hygiene rules, including jewellery and plasters,
- illness reporting and visitor rules,
- protective clothing and laundering.

## Module 6. The audit

### 6.1 Process
| Stage | Description |
|---|---|
| Preparation | choose a certification body, agree scope, duration and date (or unannounced option) |
| Opening meeting | scope, plan, people |
| Site inspection | production tour – a large part of the audit takes place on the floor |
| Document and record review | tracing selected batches, traceability test |
| Closing meeting | non-conformities presented |
| After the audit | evidence of closure submitted within 28 calendar days of the audit |

### 6.2 Non-conformity classification
| Class | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Critical | critical failure to comply with a food safety or legal requirement; no certificate is issued |
| Major | substantial failure to meet a requirement or serious doubt about product conformity |
| Minor | requirement not fully met but no direct threat to product safety |

### 6.3 Grades and audit frequency
The grade is based on the number and type of non-conformities (per Issue 9; verify in the current protocol):

| Grade | Non-conformities | Next audit |
|---|---|---|
| AA | up to 5 minors | 12 months |
| A | 6–10 minors | 12 months |
| B | 11–16 minors or 1 major and up to 10 minors | 12 months |
| C | 17–24 minors or 1 major and 11–16 minors or 2 majors and up to 10 minors | 6 months |
| D | 25–30 minors or 1 major and 17–24 minors or 2 majors and 11–16 minors | 6 months |
| no certificate | a critical non-conformity or more than allowed for grade D | re-audit |

### 6.4 Audit preparation – 10 points
1. Internal audit of all sections within the last 12 months, spread over at least 4 dates.
2. Non-conformities from the previous audit closed with evidence of effectiveness.
3. HACCP plan reviewed within the last 12 months.
4. Traceability test and recall test within the last 12 months.
5. Management review with current data.
6. Current risk assessments: raw materials, allergens, food defence, authenticity.
7. Food safety culture plan with evidence of delivery.
8. Complete training records and skills matrix.
9. GMP inspection of the whole site one week before the audit.
10. The team knows who accompanies the auditor and where documents are.
