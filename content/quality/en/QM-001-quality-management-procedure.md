# QM-001 Food Safety and Quality Management Procedure

## 1. Purpose
This procedure describes how [SITE NAME] plans, implements, maintains and improves its food safety and quality management system. It is the top-level document: it sets out the policy, objectives, organisation, risk management principles and links to all other procedures. It ensures that products are safe, legal, authentic and meet the specification agreed with the customer.

## 2. Scope
It covers all processes that affect product safety, legality, authenticity and quality: management, purchasing, goods receipt, storage, production, packing, dispatch, maintenance, hygiene, laboratory, product development, customer service and outsourced processes. It applies to all personnel, including temporary workers and contractors working on site.

## 3. Definitions
- **Food safety management system (FSMS)**: the set of policies, procedures, records and resources used to ensure food safety.
- **Food safety culture**: shared values, beliefs and norms that affect how employees think and behave towards food safety (requirement of Regulation (EC) No 852/2004 as amended by Regulation (EU) 2021/382).
- **Prerequisite programmes (PRPs)**: basic conditions and activities that maintain a hygienic environment (GHP/GMP).
- **HACCP plan**: the document describing hazards, control measures, critical control points and how they are controlled.
- **Management review**: periodic evaluation of system effectiveness by senior management.

## 4. Responsibilities
- Site Director: approves the policy, objectives and budget, provides resources, chairs the management review, is accountable for food safety culture.
- Quality Manager: system owner; maintains documentation, coordinates the HACCP team, audit programme, data analysis, CAPA and system training; reports to the Site Director.
- HACCP Team Leader: leads hazard analysis and verification of the HACCP plan.
- Department managers: implement requirements in their areas, assign record owners, deliver departmental objectives.
- All employees: work according to instructions and report hazards and deviations without fear of blame.

## 6. Food safety and quality policy
1. The policy is signed by the Site Director, displayed where staff can see it and explained to new employees.
2. The policy commits to: producing safe, legal and authentic food; meeting customer requirements; continual improvement; building a food safety culture; open communication of hazards.
3. The policy is reviewed at every management review.

## 7. Objectives and KPIs
1. Objectives are set at least annually. Each objective is measurable and has an owner, a deadline and a measurement method.
2. Progress is reviewed monthly at the quality meeting and at least annually at the management review.

## 9. Risk management
1. Food safety hazards are assessed by the HACCP team according to HA-001 (biological, chemical, physical, allergens, radiological).
2. Authenticity risks are assessed according to PR-015 and deliberate threats (food defence) according to PR-014.
3. Organisational risks and opportunities (e.g. loss of a key supplier, refrigeration failure, staff shortages) are kept in the organisational risk register (Appendix 1) and reviewed at management review.
4. Risk assessments are updated after every significant change and at least annually.

## 10. Change management
1. Every change that may affect product safety or quality (new product, raw material, supplier, equipment, layout, process, cleaning chemical, packaging) is raised on the change request form (Appendix 2).
2. The Quality Manager and process owners assess the impact on: HACCP, allergens, label, specifications, training, records and customer requirements.
3. The change is implemented after approval and verified afterwards. Where the contract requires it, the customer is informed before implementation.

## 11. Food safety culture
1. The Site Director approves an annual food safety culture plan with actions, deadlines and measurement methods.
2. The plan covers at least: communication (meetings, boards), training, employee involvement (reporting observations), management presence on the shop floor, recognition of good behaviour.
3. Employees can report concerns confidentially via [BOX / FORM / PHONE]. Reports are handled within [NUMBER] working days.
4. Effectiveness is assessed through surveys, KPIs (e.g. number of reports, GMP inspection results) and observation.

## 13. Monitoring, measurement and improvement
1. Process data (CCPs, PRPs, complaints, audits, testing, supplier performance) are analysed monthly to identify trends.
2. The internal audit programme is carried out according to PR-006.
3. Non-conformities and improvement opportunities are handled according to PR-003.
4. The HACCP system is verified at least annually and after every change, incident or new hazard information.

## 14. Management review
1. The review takes place at least annually with the Site Director and department managers.
2. Inputs: internal and external audit results, KPIs and objectives, complaints and customer feedback, incidents, withdrawals and non-conformities, CAPA status, HACCP verification results, supplier performance, changes in legislation and customer requirements, food safety culture assessment, resources, actions from the previous review.
3. Outputs: decisions on resources, new objectives, improvement actions with owners and deadlines, policy changes.
4. The review minutes (Appendix 3) are approved by the Site Director.

## 15. Resources and infrastructure
1. The Site Director provides sufficient trained staff, buildings, equipment, utilities and work environment.
2. Personnel requirements are described in PR-007 and infrastructure and equipment in PR-009.
3. Outsourced processes (e.g. external storage, laboratory, packing) are controlled as critical suppliers according to PR-002.
