# HA-001 Hazard Analysis and HACCP Plan

## 1. Purpose
This procedure sets out how [SITE NAME] carries out hazard analysis, determines critical control points (CCPs) and establishes how they are controlled, in line with the seven HACCP principles and twelve steps described by the Codex Alimentarius. The aim is to prevent, eliminate or reduce food safety hazards to acceptable levels.

## 2. Scope
It covers all products, product groups and processes on site, from receipt of raw materials and packaging to dispatch of finished product, including rework, outsourced processes and new products before launch.

## 3. Definitions
- **Hazard**: a biological, chemical or physical agent in food (including allergens) with the potential to cause an adverse health effect.
- **CCP (critical control point)**: a step at which a control measure is essential to prevent or eliminate a hazard or reduce it to an acceptable level, and for which a critical limit has been set.
- **OPRP (operational prerequisite programme)**: a control measure for a significant hazard that does not meet CCP criteria but requires monitoring (ISO 22000 concept).
- **Critical limit**: a measurable value that separates acceptable from unacceptable.
- **Monitoring**: a planned sequence of observations or measurements to assess whether a CCP is under control.
- **Validation**: obtaining evidence that a control measure, properly implemented, is capable of controlling the hazard.
- **Verification**: confirming that the HACCP plan is working as intended.

## 4. Responsibilities
- Site Director: appoints the HACCP team, provides resources, approves the HACCP plan.
- HACCP Team Leader: leads the team, maintains documentation, organises verification; has documented HACCP training.
- HACCP team members (quality, production, maintenance, technical, warehouse, external experts where needed): contribute knowledge of process, equipment and hazards.
- CCP operators: carry out monitoring, record results, react immediately to deviations.
- Quality Manager: verifies CCP records, approves corrective actions and product decisions.

## 6. Steps to develop the HACCP plan

### Step 1. HACCP team
1. The Site Director appoints the team in writing (Appendix 1). The team has competence in technology, microbiology, hygiene, equipment and legal requirements.
2. The team defines the scope of the plan: products, processes and hazards covered.

### Step 2. Product description
A description is prepared for each product or product group (Appendix 2): composition, parameters (aw, pH, salt, sugar, preservatives), processing (e.g. pasteurisation, baking), packaging and atmosphere, storage conditions, shelf life, distribution, legal and customer requirements.

### Step 3. Intended use
Define how the product will be used (eaten as is or after cooking), consumer groups and vulnerable groups (children, elderly, allergic consumers, pregnant women) and foreseeable misuse.

### Step 4. Flow diagram
The diagram covers all steps: receipt, storage, preparation, processing, packing, finished goods storage, dispatch, rework, inputs of utilities (water, steam, compressed air, gases) and packaging, waste and outsourced processes.

### Step 5. On-site confirmation
The team checks the diagram on the shop floor during operation, across all shifts, and records the date and signatures. Confirmation is repeated at least annually and after every process change.

### Step 6 (principle 1). Hazard analysis
1. For each step the team lists hazards that may reasonably occur:
   - biological: pathogenic bacteria (e.g. Salmonella, Listeria monocytogenes, STEC E. coli, Clostridium botulinum and perfringens, Bacillus cereus, Staphylococcus aureus and its toxins), viruses, parasites, moulds,
   - chemical: residues of cleaning chemicals and lubricants, pesticides, mycotoxins, heavy metals, acrylamide, migration from packaging, unauthorised additives,
   - allergens: intentionally added and cross-contact,
   - physical: metal, glass, hard plastic, wood, stones, bones, personal items.
2. Each hazard is assessed by likelihood and severity (Appendix 3, 5×5 matrix).
3. Hazards scoring ≥ [THRESHOLD, e.g. 12] are significant and need a control measure. Hazards below the threshold are controlled by PRPs.
4. A control measure is identified for each significant hazard (e.g. pasteurisation, metal detection, sieving, chilling).

### Step 7 (principle 2). Determining CCPs
1. The team applies the decision tree (Appendix 4) to each significant hazard.
2. A step is a CCP when control is essential at that step and a later step will not eliminate the hazard.
3. The rationale is recorded for each hazard.

### Step 8 (principle 3). Critical limits
1. The limit must be measurable and based on legislation, scientific literature, industry guidance or validation studies.
2. Examples (to be confirmed by on-site validation):

| Control measure | Example critical limit | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Milk pasteurisation (HTST) | at least 72°C for 15 s or equivalent | Regulation (EC) No 853/2004 |
| Cooking of cooked meat products | core temperature ≥ [e.g. 72°C] | validation, industry guidance |
| Cooling after cooking | core temperature ≤ [value] within ≤ [time] | validation |
| Metal detector | rejection of Fe [mm], non-Fe [mm], SS [mm] test pieces in the centre of the pack | customer specification, validation |
| Sieving | mesh size [mm], sieve intact | risk assessment |

3. An operational limit (tighter) may be set in addition to the critical limit, so that action is taken before the critical limit is breached.

### Step 9 (principle 4). Monitoring
For each CCP define: what is measured, the method, frequency, responsible person and record. Monitoring must detect loss of control in time to secure the product.

### Step 10 (principle 5). Corrective actions
For each CCP define in advance:
1. how process control is restored,
2. how product made since the last acceptable check is handled (hold, assess, reprocess, dispose),
3. who is authorised to decide on the product,
4. how the action is recorded and the root cause analysed according to PR-003.

### Step 11 (principle 6). Verification and validation
1. Validation: before implementation and after any change to process, product or equipment.
2. Verification: review of CCP records by the Quality Manager [FREQUENCY, e.g. daily]; calibration of instruments (PR-009); product testing (PR-017); audit of the HACCP plan at least annually; analysis of complaints and incidents.
3. The HACCP plan is reviewed at least annually and always after: changes to recipe, raw material, process, equipment or packaging; an incident or withdrawal; new hazard information (e.g. RASFF, legislative change).

### Step 12 (principle 7). Documentation
Documentation includes: team membership, product descriptions, confirmed flow diagrams, hazard analysis, CCP rationale, HACCP plan (Appendix 5), monitoring, corrective action, validation and verification records.

## 7. Link to prerequisite programmes
The HACCP plan only works on a foundation of effective PRPs: building and equipment hygiene, cleaning and disinfection (IN-001), pest control (PR-008), personal hygiene (PR-007), supplier control (PR-002), allergens (PR-005), maintenance and calibration (PR-009), water supply, glass and hard plastic control, waste management. If a PRP fails, the hazard analysis must be reviewed.

## 8. Critical limit deviation
1. The operator immediately stops or diverts the process and informs the supervisor and the Quality Manager.
2. Product since the last acceptable check is placed on hold and labelled according to PR-010.
3. The cause is removed and the CCP checked before restarting.
4. The Quality Manager decides on the product and records the decision and rationale.
5. If product has left the site, PR-013 is initiated.
