## 3. Hazard categories
- Biological: pathogens, viruses, parasites, microbial growth.
- Chemical: residues, contaminants, incorrect additive dosing.
- Physical: metal, glass, hard plastic, stones, wood and other foreign bodies.
- Allergen: undeclared presence, cross-contact, incorrect declaration.
- Authenticity: substitution, adulteration or misleading claims.
- Intentional: deliberate contamination or product-security breach.

## 4. Assessment method
For every step assess:
- Likelihood: 1 low / 2 medium / 3 high
- Severity: 1 minor / 2 significant / 3 severe
- Risk score = L x S

Example interpretation:
- 1-2 low
- 3-4 medium
- 6-9 high

The company must approve and adapt thresholds for its products and consumers.

## 6. Control determination
For significant hazards define effective control measures. Where loss of control can directly lead to unacceptable risk and no later step can reliably remove that risk, consider establishing a critical control point.

## 7. Limits, monitoring and actions
For critical controls define:
- measurable or unambiguous limit,
- method and frequency of monitoring,
- responsibility,
- action after deviation,
- product disposition,
- verification.

## 8. Validation and verification
Validate before implementation and after significant changes. Verification can include record review, audits, test results, deviations, complaints and on-site confirmation of the process flow.

## 9. Review
At least annually and following significant product, raw-material, process, equipment or legal change, or after an incident.
