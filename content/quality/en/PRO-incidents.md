# PR-013 Incident and Crisis Management

## 1. Purpose
This procedure sets out how [SITE NAME] prepares for incidents threatening food safety, legality, authenticity or continuity of supply, how it responds, communicates with authorities, customers and media, restores normal operation and learns lessons.

## 2. Scope
It covers internal and external incidents, including:
- product safety threats on the market (pathogen, allergen, foreign body, chemical contamination),
- deliberate contamination, extortion, product tampering,
- utility failures (power, water, refrigeration, gas), fire, flood, IT failure or cyberattack,
- sudden loss of a key supplier, material or staff, pandemic,
- official controls with a decision to stop production, negative media coverage.

## 3. Definitions
- **Incident**: an event that may lead to a threat to product safety or legality, interruption of supply or reputational damage.
- **Crisis**: an incident requiring the crisis team and management decisions.
- **Business continuity plan**: pre-planned alternatives for critical resources.
- **RASFF**: the EU Rapid Alert System for Food and Feed.

## 4. Responsibilities
- Site Director: chairs the crisis team, makes decisions, approves external communications.
- Quality Manager: coordinates product risk assessment, traceability and contact with official authorities; maintains documentation.
- Maintenance Manager: contingency plans for utilities and equipment.
- Sales / Logistics Manager: customer contact, product collection.
- Communications person (designated): media contact using approved messages; other staff do not speak to media.
- All employees: report incidents immediately to their supervisor or the site emergency number.

## 5. References
- Regulation (EC) No 178/2002, Article 19 (operator obligations), Article 50 (RASFF).
- [NATIONAL FOOD SAFETY AND CRISIS MANAGEMENT LEGISLATION].
- BRCGS Food Safety Issue 9, clause 3.11 (incident management, product withdrawal and recall); IFS Food version 8, clause 5.9.
- PR-004, PR-010, PR-011, PR-014.

## 6. Preparation
1. The crisis team and 24/7 contacts are in PR-004, section 9.2. The list is updated every [e.g. 6 months] and after any staff change.
2. External contacts (Appendix 1): competent food authority, police, fire service, laboratory, utility providers, critical service engineers, insurer, legal adviser, certification body, key customers.
3. Business continuity plan (Appendix 2) for critical resources, e.g.:

| Resource | Scenario | Contingency | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| Electricity | power cut | generator [capacity], priority supply to cold stores and servers, manual temperature recording | Maintenance Manager |
| Potable water | interruption, network contamination | stop production, information from water company, testing before restart, bottled water for hand washing | Maintenance / Quality |
| Refrigeration | compressor failure | 24 h service, move stock to backup or hired cold store, product temperature monitoring | Maintenance Manager |
| IT and data | failure, cyberattack | backups, paper forms, system restore | IT Administrator |
| Key raw material | supply failure | approved backup supplier (PR-002), safety stock | Purchasing Manager |
| Staff | mass absence | skills matrix, reduced range | Production Manager |

4. Instructions for common scenarios (fire, evacuation, chemical spill, glass breakage, refrigeration failure) are displayed at workplaces.

## 7. Reporting and initial assessment
1. The person detecting the incident informs the supervisor, who immediately notifies the Quality Manager or Site Director.
2. Within [e.g. 1 hour] the Quality Manager makes an initial assessment:
   - is human safety at risk (protecting life and health first: evacuation, emergency services),
   - is product on site or on the market at risk,
   - what is the scope (products, batches, customers),
   - should the crisis team be convened.
3. The incident is recorded in the incident log (Appendix 3) from the first minute: times, facts, decisions, people.

## 8. Crisis team actions
1. **Containment**: stop production, hold products (PR-010), secure evidence and samples.
2. **Facts**: traceability (PR-004), test results, process records, information from customers and authorities.
3. **Risk assessment**: nature of the hazard, likelihood, exposed groups, quantity on the market.
4. **Decision**: monitor / internal hold / withdrawal / recall (PR-004, section 9).
5. **Notifications**:
   - competent authority immediately where product may be injurious to health or does not meet safety requirements,
   - trade customers,
   - certification body within the timeframe required by the standard,
   - police where criminal activity is suspected (extortion, deliberate contamination),
   - insurer.
6. **Communication**: one message approved by the Site Director; consumer messages agreed with the authority. Internally, staff are told what to do and what not to say.
7. **Remedial action** and restart once the cause is confirmed removed (e.g. test results, cleaning, repair).

## 9. Media communication rules
1. Only the designated person speaks. Staff refer journalists to that person.
2. Messages are factual: what happened, what the site is doing, what consumers should do, where to get information. No speculation or blame.
3. A log of publications and media enquiries is kept.

## 10. Closure and lessons learned
1. The Site Director closes the incident after actions are completed and product reconciled.
2. Within [e.g. 2 weeks] the team prepares a final report (Appendix 4): timeline, root cause (PR-003), costs, evaluation of the team and procedures, lessons.
3. Lessons are fed into procedures, the HACCP plan, the business continuity plan and training.

## 11. Testing
1. At least annually the procedure is tested: incident or withdrawal simulation (may be combined with PR-004), with different scenarios in successive years.
2. The test checks contacts, time to convene the team, data availability and message preparation.
3. Results and lessons are recorded; weaknesses are handled through CAPA.

## 12. Deviations
| Deviation | Action | Decision by |
|---|---|---|
| Team member unreachable | contact deputy, update list | Quality Manager |
| Delayed notification of authority | notify immediately with explanation, root cause | Site Director |
| Employee spoke to media | discussion, reminder of rules, corrective communication | Site Director |
| Generator failed to start in test | repair, retest, review plan | Maintenance Manager |
| Data unavailable during incident (IT) | paper forms, restore from backup | IT Administrator |
