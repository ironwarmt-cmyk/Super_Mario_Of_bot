# PR-003 Non-conformities, Root Cause Analysis and Corrective Action (CAPA)

## 1. Purpose
This procedure sets out how [SITE NAME] reports, records, assesses and resolves non-conformities, identifies their root cause and implements corrective and preventive actions so that problems do not recur. It also ensures that effectiveness is verified rather than merely declared.

## 2. Scope
It covers non-conformities identified in:
- CCP and PRP monitoring, goods-in checks, laboratory testing,
- internal audits, customer and certification audits, official controls,
- customer and consumer complaints,
- incidents, withdrawals and near misses,
- trend analysis and management review.

## 3. Definitions
- **Non-conformity**: failure to meet a requirement (legal, standard, customer, internal procedure).
- **Correction**: immediate action to remove the effect (e.g. sorting product, re-cleaning).
- **Corrective action**: action to remove the cause of a detected non-conformity to prevent recurrence.
- **Preventive action**: action to remove the cause of a potential non-conformity (e.g. on another line or site).
- **Root cause**: the underlying cause which, once removed, stops the problem recurring.
- **Effectiveness**: evidence that the non-conformity does not recur after actions are implemented.

## 4. Responsibilities
- Every employee: reports non-conformities to their supervisor as soon as they are found.
- Shift supervisor: carries out correction, secures product, opens the report.
- Quality Manager: maintains the CAPA register, classifies non-conformities, assigns owners, approves root cause analysis and action plans, verifies effectiveness.
- Action owner (area manager): leads root cause analysis with the team and implements actions on time.
- Site Director: provides resources and reviews CAPA status at management review.

## 6. Reporting and recording
1. Non-conformities are reported verbally to the supervisor and then entered in the CAPA register (Appendix 1) within [e.g. 1 shift] of detection.
2. The report includes: date, location, facts (what, where, when, how much), source of detection, product and batch details, correction carried out.
3. A sequential number CAPA-[YEAR]-[NO] is assigned.

## 7. Classification and deadlines
| Class | Criteria | Examples | Root cause and plan deadline | Implementation deadline |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Critical | direct threat to product safety or legality; product may have reached the customer | CCP limit exceeded with product released, undeclared allergen on label | 24 hours (immediate containment) | [e.g. 7 days] |
| Major | significant failure to meet a requirement that may lead to a hazard | missing CCP records for one shift, ineffective cleaning at allergen changeover | [e.g. 7 days] | [e.g. 28 days] |
| Minor | isolated failure without safety impact | incomplete entry, minor floor damage outside production | [e.g. 14 days] | [e.g. 60 days] |

Deadlines for certification audit non-conformities are set by the standard and certification body and take precedence over this table.

## 8. Correction and product containment
1. The supervisor immediately removes the effect and secures suspect product according to PR-010.
2. The scope is defined from the last confirmed acceptable condition to the time of correction.
3. Where product may have left the site, the Quality Manager assesses whether to initiate PR-013 (incident, withdrawal).

## 9. Root cause analysis
1. For critical and major non-conformities, analysis is carried out by a team: area owner, Quality Manager, people involved, maintenance where relevant.
2. Methods are chosen to fit the problem:
   - **5 Whys**: "why?" is asked until the answer points to a cause the site can permanently remove (usually in the system, not the person),
   - **Ishikawa diagram** (6M: man, machine, method, material, measurement, milieu/environment) for problems with many possible causes,
   - **data and trend analysis** (e.g. loggers, test results, complaints).
3. "Human error" is not a root cause. Establish why the error was possible: training, instructions, workload, workstation design, lack of technical safeguards.
4. Root cause analysis is recorded on the CAPA form (Appendix 2) with evidence.

### 9.1 5 Whys example
| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Problem | A fragment of blue plastic was found in a batch of biscuits. |
| 1. Why? | The fragment came from a cracked mixing paddle. |
| 2. Why did the paddle crack? | It was used despite visible cracks. |
| 3. Why was it not removed? | Tool condition checks did not include paddles. |
| 4. Why not? | The hard plastics register was not updated when new tools were bought. |
| 5. Why? | Tool purchasing was not covered by change management. |
| Root cause | No link between tool purchasing, the hard plastics register and change management. |
| Actions | add tools to the register, daily checks, switch to detectable tools, change purchasing procedure. |

## 10. Action plan
1. For each cause, actions are defined with an owner, deadline and expected result.
2. The Quality Manager approves the plan and checks that actions do not create new hazards (e.g. changing a cleaning chemical and allergens).
3. Preventive actions in similar areas (other lines, products) are considered.
4. Where an action changes a document, process or HACCP, change management (QM-001) and document control (PR-001) apply.

## 11. Effectiveness verification
1. After implementation, the owner provides evidence (photos, records, test results, training records).
2. The Quality Manager assesses effectiveness after [e.g. 30–90 days] against a specific criterion set in the plan (e.g. "no recurrence in the next 3 GMP audits", "100% complete records for 4 weeks").
3. Ineffective action: repeat root cause analysis and a new plan; the CAPA remains open.
4. The Quality Manager closes the CAPA with signature and date.

## 12. Trend analysis
1. Monthly, the Quality Manager analyses the CAPA register: number, classes, areas, recurrence, timeliness.
2. Recurring non-conformities (e.g. the same issue ≥ [e.g. 3] times per quarter) are treated as major even if individually minor.
3. Results are presented at the quality meeting and management review.

## 13. Deviations
| Deviation | Action | Decision by |
|---|---|---|
| CAPA overdue | escalate to Site Director, new deadline with justification | Quality Manager |
| Analysis points only to "operator error" | reject analysis, repeat with the team | Quality Manager |
| Recurrence of a closed non-conformity | reopen CAPA, escalate class | Quality Manager |
| No resources to implement | decision on resources or temporary containment | Site Director |

## 14. Process verification
1. The internal audit (PR-006) samples closed CAPAs for quality of analysis and evidence of effectiveness.
2. "% of CAPA closed on time" is a quality objective (QM-001).
