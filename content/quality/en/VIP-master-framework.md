# VIP-001 Food Safety and Quality Management Framework

## 1. Purpose
This document is the overarching framework for sites that want to build and maintain a complete system meeting EU food law and the requirements of BRCGS Food Safety, IFS Food or ISO 22000/FSSC 22000. It ties all package procedures into one working system: with a compliance calendar, KPIs, maturity assessment, requirements for areas not covered by separate procedures (site layout, utilities, process control, high-care and high-risk zones) and rules for tailoring documents to a specific site.

## 2. Scope
It applies to the whole of [SITE NAME] and all QM, HA, PR and IN documents. It is intended for the Site Director, Quality Manager and HACCP team.

## 3. Definitions
- **Maturity assessment**: evaluation of how far the system is implemented and effective, on a scale from 1 (absent) to 5 (data-driven improvement).
- **High-care area**: area for products that have been treated to reduce microorganisms to a safe level but remain vulnerable to pathogen growth and are eaten without further treatment (BRCGS terminology).
- **High-risk area**: area for products fully treated to eliminate vegetative pathogens, ready to eat and vulnerable to pathogen growth (BRCGS terminology).
- **Process control**: defined parameters, limits and monitoring ensuring a consistent, compliant product.

## 4. Responsibilities
- Site Director: management-level system owner, food safety culture, resources, management review.
- Quality Manager: coordination, compliance calendar, KPIs, audits, reporting.
- HACCP Team Leader: food safety plan, validation and verification.
- Production, maintenance, warehouse, purchasing and HR managers: deliver duties assigned in the calendar.

## 7. Site layout, flows and infrastructure
1. The site plan (site-specific document) shows: hygiene zones, flows of raw materials, personnel, waste, packaging, rework and finished products, transfer points (hygiene barriers), hand wash stations, allergen and chemical storage zones.
2. Flow principles: from raw material to finished product without back-tracking, no crossing of raw and finished product routes, and where physical separation is not possible – separation in time with cleaning in between.
3. Building: floors sealed, washable and draining to drains; walls and ceilings smooth, light-coloured, washable; lighting with covers; opening windows screened; doors sealed.
4. Utilities: water for product contact and cleaning is potable, confirmed by the testing plan (PR-017) and a water system plan; steam and ice in contact with product made from potable water; compressed air and gases in contact with product filtered and periodically tested (oil, particles, microorganisms) per risk assessment.
5. Ventilation: sufficient to remove steam and condensation; airflow from cleaner to less clean areas; in high-risk areas positive pressure and filtration (BRCGS section 8).

## 8. Process control
1. Each product has a process specification: parameters (temperature, time, speed, pressure, weight), limits and monitoring.
2. Equipment settings are protected from unauthorised change (passwords, locks).
3. The first batch after start-up, product change or breakdown is checked before continuing (start-up check).
4. Alarms and breakdowns (e.g. oven power loss, refrigeration failure) have defined handling of work-in-progress.
5. Net quantity is controlled per prepacked goods legislation (PR-012).
6. Critical processes are validated at introduction and after change (HA-001, PR-016).

## 9. High-care and high-risk zones
This applies to sites making ready-to-eat products vulnerable to pathogen growth (e.g. sandwiches, salads, sliced cooked meats, ready meals). The HACCP team assesses whether such zones are required.

| Element | Requirement (summary) |
|---|---|
| Segregation | physical barriers from lower-risk areas, dedicated entrances via hygiene barriers |
| Personnel | dedicated distinctively coloured clothing, footwear change or barrier bench, hand washing and sanitising at entry, limited headcount |
| Air | filtration and positive pressure relative to adjacent areas, air changes per risk assessment |
| Equipment and tools | dedicated, colour-coded; items from outside enter only after cleaning and disinfection |
| Cleaning | dedicated cleaning equipment, validated programmes, microbiological verification |
| Environmental monitoring | swabbing programme for Listeria spp. (and other pathogens per assessment) in all zones (PR-017) |
| Product flow | product enters after treatment via a pass-through (e.g. oven, chiller tunnel) or a hatch with pack disinfection |
| Waste | removed without passing through lower-risk areas in a way that risks contamination |

## 12. System maturity assessment
Annually, before management review, the team scores each pillar (section 6):

| Level | Description |
|---|---|
| 1 | No documents or practice. |
| 2 | Documents exist but are not applied consistently. |
| 3 | Documents are applied and records complete; problems fixed ad hoc. |
| 4 | System is effective; root causes removed permanently; KPIs monitored. |
| 5 | Data- and trend-driven improvement; employees actively propose improvements; management regularly present on the floor. |

The result and improvement plan are recorded on the assessment sheet (Appendix 1).

## 13. Preparing for an external audit
1. [e.g. 8 weeks] before: full internal audit, gap analysis against the current version of the standard, review of customer requirements.
2. [e.g. 4 weeks] before: non-conformities closed or documented plans, traceability test, review of records for the last 3–12 months.
3. [e.g. 1 week] before: GMP inspection of the whole site, staff reminders, documents prepared (register, HACCP plan, test results, management review minutes).
4. On the day: designated guide, access to documents, notes of findings.
5. After: action plan within the timeframe required by the standard, closure evidence sent to the certification body.

## 14. Tailoring documents to the site
Every document prepared for a specific company takes into account at least:
- products and their intended use, including vulnerable groups,
- target market and applicable law (EU, national, customer requirements),
- process and equipment, including CCPs,
- raw materials, their origin and allergens,
- site layout and hygiene zones,
- traceability and batch coding system,
- testing plan and laboratories,
- organisational structure and responsibilities,
- requirements of the chosen certification standard.
