## Learning objectives
After the training the participant:
- explains what IFS Food is and how certification works,
- knows the structure of version 8 and the KO requirements,
- understands the scoring system and certification levels,
- can identify evidence of implementation for key requirements,
- can prepare an action plan after an audit,
- knows the differences in approach between IFS and BRCGS.

## Module 1. Introduction

### 1.1 What IFS Food is
IFS (International Featured Standards) Food is a food safety and quality standard for manufacturers and companies packing loose food products. It was created by German and French retail associations and is GFSI-benchmarked. It is particularly widespread among retail suppliers in Germany, France, Italy, Poland and many other European countries.

Version 8 was published in April 2023. Audits against version 8 have been mandatory since 1 January 2024. Version 8 contains 234 requirements in five chapters (the former food defence chapter was moved into chapter 4).

### 1.2 Version 8 structure
| Chapter | Title | Example topics |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Governance and commitment | policy, management responsibility, food safety culture, management review |
| 2 | Food safety and quality management system | documents (2.1.1), records (2.1.2), food safety management (2.2), HACCP analysis – 12 steps (2.3) |
| 3 | Resource management | human resources (3.1), personal hygiene (3.2), training (3.3), staff facilities (3.4) |
| 4 | Operational processes | customer requirements (4.1), specifications and formulas (4.2), product development (4.3), purchasing (4.4), packaging (4.5), location, exterior, layout and premises (4.6–4.9), cleaning (4.10), waste (4.11), foreign material and chemical risk (4.12), pests (4.13), receipt and storage (4.14), transport (4.15), maintenance (4.16), equipment (4.17), traceability (4.18), allergens (4.19), food fraud (4.20), food defence (4.21) |
| 5 | Measurements, analyses, improvements | internal audits (5.1), site inspections (5.2), process validation and control (5.3), calibration (5.4), quantity control (5.5), product testing and environmental monitoring (5.6), product release (5.7), complaints (5.8), recalls and incidents (5.9), non-conforming products (5.10), corrective actions (5.11) |

### 1.3 Characteristic features of IFS
- strong focus on compliance with **customer requirements** (recipe, process, label, specifications) – a KO requirement,
- **scoring** of every requirement,
- "product and process" audit: the auditor follows the product through the whole site,
- **unannounced audits** receive "Star" status; IFS is gradually increasing the share of unannounced audits – rules are set by the current certification protocol.

## Module 2. Scoring and KO requirements

### 2.1 Requirement scoring
| Score | Meaning | Points |
|---|---|---|
| A | full compliance | 20 |
| B | deviation – almost full compliance | 15 |
| C | only a small part of the requirement implemented | 5 |
| D | requirement not implemented | −20 |
| NA | not applicable (with justification) | not counted |

In addition:
- **major non-conformity**: where there is a substantial food safety risk or significant failure to meet a requirement; deducts 15% of the possible total and requires an additional audit; no certificate is issued,
- **KO requirement scored D**: deducts 50% of the total; no certificate is issued. KO requirements can only be scored A, B or D (not C).

Point values and calculation rules must be confirmed in the current "IFS Scoring system" brochure and certification protocol.

### 2.2 KO (knock-out) requirements in version 8
| KO | Requirement | Topic |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1.2.1 | management responsibility: employees are aware of their food safety and quality responsibilities and their performance is monitored |
| 2 | 2.3.9.1 | monitoring of each CCP with records |
| 3 | 3.2.2 | personal hygiene requirements understood and applied by staff, contractors and visitors |
| 4 | 4.1.3 | compliance with customer agreements (recipe, process, testing, packaging, labelling) |
| 5 | 4.2.1.3 | specifications for raw materials, including packaging and rework |
| 6 | 4.12.1 | foreign material and chemical risk mitigation |
| 7 | 4.18.1 | traceability system |
| 8 | 5.1.1 | internal audit programme |
| 9 | 5.9.1 | recall, withdrawal, incident and emergency procedure |
| 10 | 5.11.3 | corrective actions |

### 2.3 Result and certification levels
| Result | Outcome |
|---|---|
| no KO scored D, no major, score ≥ 95% | certificate at Higher Level |
| no KO scored D, no major, score ≥ 75% and < 95% | certificate at Foundation Level |
| major non-conformity or score < 75% | no certificate; additional or new audit required |
| KO scored D | no certificate; new audit |

### 2.4 Exercise 1 – calculating the score
A site had 200 scored requirements: 180 × A, 15 × B, 4 × C, 1 × D (not KO), no majors.
- Possible points: 200 × 20 = 4000.
- Achieved: 180 × 20 + 15 × 15 + 4 × 5 + 1 × (−20) = 3600 + 225 + 20 − 20 = 3825.
- Score: 3825 / 4000 = 95.6% → Higher Level.

Calculate yourself: how does the score change if two A scores become D?

## Module 3. Governance, system and HACCP (chapters 1–2)

### 3.1 Governance and culture
| Requirement | Example evidence |
|---|---|
| Corporate policy and objectives | policy including food safety culture elements, objectives with measures |
| Structure and responsibilities | organisation chart, job descriptions, deputies |
| Employee awareness (KO 1) | staff interviews, training, observations, monitoring of effectiveness |
| Management review | at least annually, with data and decisions |
| Legal compliance | system for tracking legal changes in countries of production and sale |

### 3.2 HACCP under IFS (2.2–2.3)
IFS mirrors the 12 Codex Alimentarius steps. The audit focuses on:
- validation of critical limits (2.3.8.1),
- CCP monitoring with records signed by responsible persons (KO 2),
- corrective actions on deviation (2.3.10),
- HACCP plan verification at least annually or after changes (2.3.11).

### 3.3 Documents and records (2.1)
Records must be legible, genuine, protected from unauthorised change and kept for the product shelf life plus at least one year (or longer where law requires). Electronic systems need access control and backups.

## Module 4. Human resources and hygiene (chapter 3)
| Area | What the auditor looks at |
|---|---|
| Personal hygiene risk assessment | whether rules (hair, jewellery, nails, eating) are based on risk assessment |
| Application of rules (KO 3) | whether staff, contractors and visitors actually follow the rules – observation on the floor |
| Protective clothing | cleanliness, changing, laundering, rules for toilets and off-site |
| Illness | reporting procedure, exclusion decisions |
| Training | programme, records, effectiveness assessment, training in an understood language |
| Staff facilities | changing rooms, toilets, hand washing, hygiene locks |

## Module 5. Operational processes (chapter 4)

### 5.1 Customer requirements and specifications
- Procedure for establishing and communicating customer requirements to the relevant departments (4.1).
- Product compliance with customer agreements (KO 4): recipe, process, testing plan, packaging, label.
- Specifications for all raw materials, packaging and rework, current and available (KO 5).
- Finished product specifications compliant with law and customer agreements.

### 5.2 Purchasing and suppliers (4.4)
Raw material risk assessment, supplier approval and monitoring, assessment of services and outsourced processes.

### 5.3 Foreign material and chemical risk (4.12, KO 6)
- risk assessment for foreign material and chemical contamination,
- management of glass, brittle plastics, wood and sharp tools,
- detection equipment (metal detector, X-ray) with defined sensitivity and testing,
- control of chemicals and lubricants.

### 5.4 Traceability (4.18, KO 7)
The system covers raw materials, packaging, rework, processes and customers. A traceability test is performed at least annually, with quantity reconciliation; results must be available within 4 hours (check the current wording of the requirement).

### 5.5 Allergens, food fraud, food defence (4.19–4.21)
| Area | Key elements |
|---|---|
| Allergens (4.19) | risk assessment, segregation, cleaning, labels, justified "may contain" statements |
| Food fraud (4.20) | raw material vulnerability assessment, mitigation plan, review at least annually |
| Food defence (4.21) | threat assessment, protection plan, access control, testing |

### 5.6 Exercise 2 – customer compliance
Choose one product for a key customer. Compare: customer specification, production recipe, label and testing plan. Record every discrepancy. Each discrepancy is a potential D score for KO 4.

## Module 6. Measurements, analysis, improvement (chapter 5)

### 6.1 Internal audits and inspections (5.1–5.2, KO 8)
- audit programme covering all IFS requirements, risk-based,
- areas critical to food safety audited at least annually,
- competent and independent auditors,
- site inspections (hygiene, fabrication) at risk-based frequency.

### 6.2 Process control, calibration, quantity (5.3–5.5)
Process validation, revalidation after changes, calibration with status identification, net quantity control compliant with law.

### 6.3 Testing and release (5.6–5.7)
Risk-based testing plan, tests performed by ISO/IEC 17025-accredited laboratories or using validated methods, environmental monitoring, product release procedure.

### 6.4 Complaints, recalls, non-conforming product, CAPA (5.8–5.11)
| Requirement | Key elements |
|---|---|
| Complaints (5.8) | recording, analysis, actions, trends |
| Recalls and incidents (5.9, KO 9) | procedure, team, contacts, test at least annually, informing the certification body and IFS within the required time |
| Non-conforming product (5.10) | identification, isolation, decision |
| Corrective actions (5.11, KO 10) | root cause analysis, actions, deadlines, effectiveness verification |

## Module 7. Audit and action plan

### 7.1 Audit process
| Stage | Description |
|---|---|
| Preparation | choose a certification body, agree scope (product and technology categories), duration and option (announced / unannounced) |
| Opening meeting | plan, scope |
| Floor audit | tracing product, observing practices, staff interviews |
| Documents | record review, traceability test |
| Closing meeting | presentation of (preliminary) deviations and non-conformities |
| Action plan | the site submits a corrective action plan with corrections, root cause analysis and deadlines within the time set in the protocol (usually 2 weeks of receiving the preliminary report) |

### 7.2 Writing the action plan
Each item should include:
1. **Correction**: what was done immediately to remove the effect.
2. **Root cause**: why it happened (with evidence of analysis).
3. **Corrective action**: what we change so it does not recur.
4. **Responsible person and deadline.**
5. **How effectiveness will be verified.**

| Deviation | Correction | Root cause | Corrective action | Who | Deadline |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| No hygiene risk assessment for hair accessories | risk assessment completed, ban introduced | hygiene procedure not reviewed after standard change | review of procedure compliance with new standard version added to audit programme | Quality Manager | [date] |

### 7.3 IFS vs. BRCGS – key differences
| Aspect | IFS Food | BRCGS Food Safety |
|---|---|---|
| Scoring | points, percentage score | number and class of non-conformities, grades AA–D |
| Key requirements | 10 KO requirements | fundamental requirements |
| Levels | Higher / Foundation | grades AA, A, B, C, D ("+" for unannounced) |
| Strong focus | customer compliance, recipe | culture, management commitment, production zones |
| Frequency | every 12 months | 12 or 6 months depending on grade |
