# PR-006 Internal Audits and GMP Inspections

## 1. Purpose
This procedure sets out how [SITE NAME] plans, conducts and documents internal system audits and hygiene (GMP) inspections, to independently confirm that the food safety and quality system is effectively implemented and meets legal, standard and customer requirements, and to identify areas for improvement.

## 2. Scope
It covers all processes and areas of the site, the HACCP plan, prerequisite programmes, support processes (purchasing, maintenance, HR, IT) and outsourced processes. It applies to scheduled audits, additional audits (after an incident, change or at management's request) and GMP inspections.

## 3. Definitions
- **Internal audit**: a systematic, independent and documented examination of whether activities and results meet requirements.
- **GMP inspection**: a short, frequent check of hygiene, building and equipment condition and staff practices on the shop floor.
- **Audit criteria**: requirements used for comparison (procedures, legislation, standard, customer specification).
- **Audit evidence**: records, observations and statements that can be verified.
- **Observation**: a situation that is not a non-conformity but could lead to one.

## 4. Responsibilities
- Quality Manager: prepares the annual audit programme, assigns auditors, oversees completion, keeps the results register, reports to the Site Director.
- Internal auditor: prepares, conducts the audit, documents evidence, raises non-conformities, verifies actions.
- Auditee area manager: provides access, attends the closing meeting, develops and implements corrective actions.
- Site Director: provides auditors' time and resources and reviews results.

## 5. References
- BRCGS Food Safety Issue 9, clause 3.4 (internal audits, including hygiene and fabrication inspections).
- IFS Food version 8, clauses 5.1 (internal audits) and 5.2 (site factory inspections).
- ISO 22000:2018, clause 9.2; ISO 19011:2018 (guidelines for auditing management systems).
- QM-001, PR-003, HA-001.

## 6. Auditor competence
1. Auditors have completed audit training (e.g. based on ISO 19011 or a BRCGS/IFS internal auditor course) and know HACCP and the standard requirements.
2. Auditors do not audit their own work or an area they are directly responsible for.
3. The Quality Manager keeps a list of auditors and their qualifications (Appendix 1) and evaluates their performance at least annually (e.g. witnessed audit, report review).
4. Where no independent auditor is available for an area (e.g. auditing the quality department), an external auditor or one from another site is used.

## 7. Planning
1. The programme covers 12 months and all system and standard requirements. Audits are spread throughout the year over at least [e.g. 4] dates (BRCGS requires audits to be spread over at least four different dates in the year).
2. Frequency is risk-based:

| Area | Risk | System audit frequency |
|---|---|---|
| HACCP plan and CCPs | high | at least annually, recommended twice |
| Allergens | high | at least annually |
| Cleaning and disinfection, personal hygiene | high | at least annually |
| Traceability, withdrawal | high | at least annually |
| Suppliers, goods-in | medium | annually |
| Maintenance, calibration | medium | annually |
| Pests, waste | medium | annually |
| Documentation, training | low | annually |

3. An area with an incident, serious complaint or external audit non-conformity is audited additionally.
4. The programme is approved by the Site Director (Appendix 2).

## 8. Conducting the audit
1. **Preparation**: the auditor reviews the area procedures, previous results, complaints and CAPAs and prepares a question list (Appendix 3).
2. **Opening meeting**: purpose, scope, criteria, schedule.
3. **Gathering evidence**: observing work on the shop floor (all shifts over the year, including night and weekend where applicable), interviewing staff, reviewing records (a sample from several days or weeks), tracing a selected batch from receipt to dispatch.
4. **Evaluation**: each piece of evidence is compared with the criterion; good practice is also recorded.
5. **Closing meeting**: findings presented, non-conformities and deadlines agreed.

## 9. Classification of findings
| Class | Criterion |
|---|---|
| Critical | direct threat to product safety or legality |
| Major | significant failure to meet a requirement or absence of an entire system element |
| Minor | isolated failure without direct product impact |
| Observation | opportunity for improvement |

Non-conformities are entered in the CAPA register (PR-003) with deadlines appropriate to the class.

## 10. Audit report
1. The report is prepared within [e.g. 5 working days] (Appendix 4). It includes: scope, date, auditor, auditees, criteria, description of evidence, non-conformities with class, strengths, conclusions.
2. Non-conformities are written as: **requirement – finding – evidence**. Example: "PR-005 clause 7.5 requires allergen test verification after cleaning following nut production; on [date] on line 2 no test was carried out (no entry on line release sheet, confirmed by operator)".

## 11. GMP inspections
1. Hygiene and fabrication inspections are carried out at least monthly in each production area (more often in high-risk zones, e.g. weekly), using a checklist (Appendix 5).
2. The inspection covers: cleanliness, building and equipment condition, glass and hard plastics, pest evidence, personal hygiene, product labelling, allergen practices, foreign body risks, warehouse order.
3. The result is expressed as a percentage of compliant items and is a KPI (QM-001). Defects are entered in the action log with owner and deadline.
4. A member of senior management takes part in inspections at least every [e.g. quarter] (part of food safety culture).

## 12. Deviations
| Deviation | Action | Decision by |
|---|---|---|
| Audit not completed on schedule | new date within [e.g. 1 month], Site Director informed | Quality Manager |
| Critical non-conformity during audit | immediate product containment, report to Quality Manager before audit ends | Quality Manager |
| No independent auditor | external auditor or auditor from another department | Quality Manager |
| Recurring GMP defects | escalate to CAPA as major | Quality Manager |

## 13. Verification
At least annually the Quality Manager checks that the programme has covered all standard requirements and presents at management review: programme completion, number and class of non-conformities, CAPA timeliness, GMP trend.
