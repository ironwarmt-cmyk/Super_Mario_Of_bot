# PR-015 Raw Material Authenticity and Food Fraud Vulnerability Assessment

## 1. Purpose
This procedure sets out how [SITE NAME] assesses raw material vulnerability to adulteration or substitution (food fraud) and implements controls so that products are authentic, match label claims (composition, origin, production method) and do not defraud consumers or customers.

## 2. Scope
It covers all raw materials, especially those with specific claims (origin, organic, protected designations, "non-GMO", species, variety) and high-value or limited-availability materials. It also covers claims on finished product labels.

## 3. Definitions
- **Food fraud**: deliberate action for economic gain involving substitution, dilution, addition, mislabelling or concealment of defects.
- **Vulnerability assessment (VACCP)**: assessment of how exposed a raw material is to fraud, considering history, economic incentive and ease of detection.
- **Specific claim**: label information requiring documentary support (e.g. "organic", "Protected Designation of Origin", "100% juice", country of origin).
- **Claim mass balance**: comparison of quantity of claim-bearing raw material purchased with quantity of finished product sold bearing that claim.

## 4. Responsibilities
- Quality Manager: carries out and updates the vulnerability assessment, plans authenticity testing, monitors information sources.
- Purchasing Manager: shares market information (prices, shortages), obtains documents supporting claims, avoids opaque supply chains.
- Technologist: verifies label claims against raw material documents.
- Site Director: approves the assessment, provides resources for testing.

## 6. Information sources
The Quality Manager monitors at least [e.g. monthly]:
- RASFF Window and reports of the EU Knowledge Centre for Food Fraud and Quality (monthly case summaries),
- communications from national authorities and trade associations,
- information from suppliers, customers and laboratories,
- market conditions: price increases, poor harvests, export restrictions, conflicts in countries of origin.

## 7. Vulnerability assessment
1. For each raw material or group, the following factors are scored (Appendix 1):

| Factor | Low vulnerability (1) | High vulnerability (5) |
|---|---|---|
| Fraud history | no known cases | frequent, well-documented cases |
| Economic incentive | low price, stable market | high price, shortages, large price gap between grades |
| Supply chain | direct from manufacturer, short | many intermediaries, high-risk countries |
| Physical form | whole, recognisable (e.g. whole fruit) | powders, oils, liquids, blends |
| Ease of detection | simple routine test | requires advanced methods |
| Supplier relationship | long-standing, audited | new supplier, spot purchases |

2. The score (average or sum per the chosen method) assigns the material to low, medium or high vulnerability.
3. Examples of materials often identified as vulnerable (confirm with current data): honey, olive oil, spices (saffron, paprika, turmeric, oregano), fruit juices, nuts and nut pastes, fish and seafood (species), meat (species), organic products, milk powder, vanilla.

## 8. Controls
| Category | Example controls |
|---|---|
| Low | standard supplier approval, specification, supplier declaration |
| Medium | as above plus GFSI certificate with food fraud module, origin documents, periodic authenticity testing [e.g. annually] |
| High | as above plus supplier audit, purchase direct from manufacturer or via verified chain, testing of every or every [x] delivery in a specialist laboratory, sealing, limited number of suppliers, mass balance |

Example test methods: sugar profiles and isotope analysis (honey, juices), fatty acid and sterol profiles (oils), DNA (meat and fish species), microscopy and illegal dyes (spices), melamine (protein). Method selection is agreed with the laboratory.

## 9. Specific claims
1. Every claim on the label must be supported by a document for each delivery (e.g. supplier's organic certificate with scope, certificate of origin, quality scheme certificate).
2. Claim-bearing materials are stored and issued so they do not mix with non-claim materials (labelling, separate location).
3. At least annually a mass balance is carried out for each claim (e.g. kg organic material purchased vs. used in organic products).
4. Processing organic products requires certification of the site by an authorised control body.

## 10. Deviations
| Deviation | Immediate action | Corrective action | Decision by |
|---|---|---|---|
| Test result indicates adulteration | hold batch and products where used | complaint, supplier suspension, assess PR-013, inform authority where required | Quality Manager, Site Director |
| No document supporting a claim | stop use with the claim | obtain document or alternative use (without claim) | Quality Manager |
| Claim mass balance does not close | stop sale with claim pending clarification | CAPA, review records | Quality Manager |
| Sudden price rise or shortage | reassess vulnerability | increased testing, supplier control | Quality Manager, purchasing |
| Offer well below market price | reject or full verification before purchase | | Purchasing Manager, quality |

## 11. Verification
The vulnerability assessment is reviewed at least annually and on new threat information. Authenticity test results and mass balances are presented at management review.
