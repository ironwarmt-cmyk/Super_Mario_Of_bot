# PR-017 Testing Plan, Laboratory and Environmental Monitoring

## 1. Purpose
This procedure sets out how [SITE NAME] plans, takes, tests and evaluates samples of raw materials, intermediates, finished products, water, surfaces and air to confirm safety, legal and specification compliance and the effectiveness of prerequisite programmes and the HACCP plan.

## 2. Scope
It covers microbiological, physico-chemical, allergen, sensory and contaminant testing performed in the on-site and external laboratories, environmental monitoring (including for Listeria), water testing and retention of samples.

## 3. Definitions
- **Testing plan**: list of what, where, how often, by which method and against which criterion testing is performed.
- **Food safety criterion**: a microbiological criterion defining the acceptability of a product or batch, applicable to products placed on the market (Regulation (EC) No 2073/2005).
- **Process hygiene criterion**: a criterion indicating acceptable functioning of the production process; exceeding it requires corrective action in the process.
- **Environmental monitoring**: planned swabbing of the production environment to detect pathogenic or indicator organisms.
- **Monitoring zones**: zone 1 – contact surfaces; zone 2 – surfaces adjacent to zone 1; zone 3 – other surfaces in the room (floors, drains); zone 4 – areas outside production.
- **Accredited laboratory**: a laboratory accredited to ISO/IEC 17025 for the method concerned.

## 4. Responsibilities
- Quality Manager: prepares and reviews the testing plan, selects laboratories, evaluates results and trends, decides on actions.
- Laboratory technician / sampler: takes, labels, transports and submits samples, performs on-site tests.
- External laboratory: tests using accredited methods, provides reports within the agreed time.
- Production Manager: provides access to sampling points, implements actions after non-conforming results.

## 6. Developing the testing plan
1. The plan (Appendix 1) is based on hazard analysis (HA-001), legal requirements (e.g. Regulation (EC) No 2073/2005 for the food category), customer requirements, raw material risk (PR-002, PR-015) and results history.
2. For each item define: subject, parameter, method, laboratory, frequency, number of samples (e.g. n = 5 for criteria with sampling plans), criterion and action on exceedance.
3. Example scope (adapt to site products):

| Subject | Parameters (examples) | Frequency (example) |
|---|---|---|
| High-risk raw materials | Salmonella, E. coli, moulds, mycotoxins, pesticides, allergens, authenticity | by risk category, e.g. every or every [x] delivery |
| Finished product | criteria from Reg. 2073/2005 for the category (e.g. Listeria monocytogenes for RTE), indicator organisms, specification parameters | [e.g. weekly per product / group] |
| Finished product – contaminants | acrylamide (bakery, fried), heavy metals, mycotoxins | [e.g. quarterly / annually] |
| "Gluten-free" or allergen-free products | gluten (≤ 20 mg/kg for "gluten-free"), allergen | [e.g. every batch / monthly] |
| Water | microbiological and physico-chemical parameters | per risk assessment, e.g. [quarterly] at outlets |
| Surfaces (hygiene) | total viable count, Enterobacteriaceae, ATP | per IN-001 |
| Environmental monitoring | Listeria spp. (RTE), Salmonella (dry products) | by zone, e.g. weekly in zones 2–3 |
| Hands | total viable count, Enterobacteriaceae, coagulase-positive staphylococci | [e.g. monthly] |
| Air (where relevant) | moulds, yeasts, total count | [e.g. quarterly] |

## 7. Environmental monitoring
1. The programme includes a sampling point plan by zone (Appendix 2), focusing on hard-to-clean, wet and high-traffic locations (drains, equipment undersides, seals, trolleys).
2. Swabs are taken during production (at least several hours after start) and periodically after cleaning, rotating to cover all points within [e.g. a quarter].
3. For ready-to-eat foods able to support growth of Listeria monocytogenes, sampling of processing areas and equipment is a legal requirement (Article 5(2) of Regulation (EC) No 2073/2005).
4. A positive result in zones 2–3 triggers intensive cleaning, re-swabbing of the point and surroundings (vector swabbing) and root cause analysis. A positive in zone 1 additionally requires assessment and hold of product made since the last negative result and product testing.

## 8. Sampling and submission
1. Samples are taken by trained staff, aseptically, per laboratory instructions and standards (e.g. ISO 18593 for surfaces).
2. Each sample is labelled: name, batch, date and time of sampling, location, sampler, purpose.
3. Microbiology samples are transported chilled (e.g. 1–8°C) and delivered within the time required by the method (usually within 24 hours).
4. Submission is documented on the request form (Appendix 3).

## 9. Laboratory selection and control
1. Tests affecting product safety and legality (pathogens, allergens, contaminants, release tests) are performed by a laboratory accredited to ISO/IEC 17025 for the method, or the method is equivalent and validated.
2. External laboratories are approved service providers per PR-002; the site checks the scope of accreditation with the national accreditation body.
3. On-site laboratory (if any):
   - is separated from production; pathogen testing is performed only in a suitably equipped laboratory with hazard controls (or not on site),
   - uses validated methods, calibrated equipment (PR-009) and trained staff,
   - takes part in proficiency testing or inter-laboratory comparisons at least annually.

## 10. Evaluation of results
1. The Quality Manager reviews each report within [e.g. 1 working day] of receipt and compares the result with the criterion.
2. Results are entered in the summary (Appendix 4) and trends analysed monthly (e.g. rising results within limits).
3. Positive release: where the plan requires a result before dispatch, the batch has "awaiting release" status until a compliant result (PR-012).

## 11. Retained samples
From each finished batch, [e.g. 2 packs] are retained under declared conditions for shelf life + [e.g. 1 month] for complaints and investigations (where product and risk assessment allow).

## 12. Deviations
| Deviation | Immediate action | Corrective action | Decision by |
|---|---|---|---|
| Food safety criterion exceeded in product | hold batch (PR-010), assess adjacent batches, PR-013 if on the market | root cause, CAPA, increased testing | Quality Manager, Site Director |
| Process hygiene criterion exceeded | review process and cleaning, retest | CAPA, improve hygiene and raw material selection | Quality Manager |
| Listeria spp. positive in zone 1 | hold product since last negative, test product, intensive clean | investigate source, vector swabbing, CAPA | Quality Manager |
| Water out of specification | stop production depending on parameter, inform water company, retest | disinfect system, review | Quality Manager, maintenance |
| Sample taken or transported incorrectly | invalidate, resample | training | Quality Manager |
| Laboratory not accredited for the method | result for information only, repeat in accredited laboratory | review service provider | Quality Manager |

## 13. Verification
The testing plan is reviewed at least annually and after changes to product, process or legislation, or after a non-conforming result. The results summary and trends are presented at management review.
