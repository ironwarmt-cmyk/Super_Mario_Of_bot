# PR-016 Product Design and Development

## 1. Purpose
This procedure sets out how [SITE NAME] introduces new products and changes existing ones (recipe, raw material, process, packaging), so that before full-scale production it is confirmed that the product is safe, legal, authentic, manufacturable on site, has a validated shelf life and a correct label.

## 2. Scope
It covers new products, line extensions (new flavour, pack size), changes to recipe, raw material or supplier, process, packaging or packaging material, storage conditions, label claims, and products made to customer specification.

## 3. Definitions
- **Product brief**: description of objectives: intended use, target consumers, market, channel, customer requirements, target price, claims.
- **Laboratory / pilot trial**: small-scale production to test recipe and process.
- **Factory trial**: first production on site lines under normal conditions.
- **Shelf-life study**: testing of product over the full declared period under storage conditions (and abuse conditions where relevant) to confirm safety and quality to the end of shelf life.
- **Release to full production**: team decision after all stages (gates) are completed.

## 4. Responsibilities
- Development Manager / technologist: leads the project, recipe, trials and documentation.
- HACCP team: assesses hazards of the new product or change and updates the HACCP plan.
- Quality Manager: approves specifications, testing plan, label and release to production.
- Production Manager: assesses manufacturability, impact on scheduling and cleaning, trains operators.
- Purchasing Manager: approves suppliers of new materials and packaging (PR-002).
- Customer: approves samples and specification where the product is for them.

## 5. References
- Regulation (EC) No 178/2002, Article 14.
- Regulation (EC) No 2073/2005, Article 3(2) and Annex II (shelf-life studies for Listeria monocytogenes in ready-to-eat foods).
- Regulation (EU) No 1169/2011; Regulation (EU) 2015/2283 (novel foods); Regulation (EC) No 1333/2008 (additives); Regulation (EC) No 1334/2008 (flavourings).
- Regulation (EU) 2017/2158 (acrylamide) for baked and fried products.

## 6. Project stages (gates)

### Gate 1. Brief and initial assessment
1. Product brief (Appendix 1).
2. Initial assessment: legal compliance (composition, additives, novel food, claims), allergens new to site, customer requirements, technical feasibility, hazards (e.g. new high-risk or ready-to-eat material).
3. Decision: proceed / change brief / stop.

### Gate 2. Recipe and trials
1. Recipe and raw material specifications from approved or assessed suppliers.
2. Laboratory or pilot trials: process parameters, sensory evaluation, preliminary testing (e.g. aw, pH, salt, microbiology).
3. Introducing an allergen new to site requires a PR-005 risk assessment before any trial on site lines.

### Gate 3. Hazard analysis and validation
1. The HACCP team carries out hazard analysis for the new product or change and determines CCPs (HA-001).
2. Validation of key control measures (e.g. heat treatment, chilling, foreign body detection) on site lines.
3. Raw material fraud vulnerability (PR-015) and food defence (PR-014) assessment where the change requires it.

### Gate 4. Factory trial
1. Trial under normal conditions, with quality and production present and full records.
2. Assessment: compliance with specification, yield, technical issues, cleaning (especially allergens), packing, net quantity.
3. Factory trial product is not sold without release like any batch (PR-012) and, where shelf-life testing is incomplete, without a Quality Manager decision based on data.

### Gate 5. Shelf life and label
1. Shelf life is set on the basis of a shelf-life study (Appendix 2) considering:
   - microbiology (relevant pathogens, indicator and spoilage organisms),
   - physico-chemical parameters (e.g. aw, pH, fat oxidation, moisture),
   - sensory evaluation,
   - packaging and storage conditions, including realistic consumer conditions and after opening.
2. For ready-to-eat foods able to support growth of Listeria monocytogenes, the requirements of Regulation (EC) No 2073/2005 apply (e.g. shelf-life studies, challenge tests or predictive modelling).
3. The label is prepared and approved per PR-012; nutrition is calculated from reliable data or tested.
4. Shelf life may be set provisionally based on similar products and literature, with a requirement to confirm by testing by a set date.

### Gate 6. Release to full production
The team confirms completion of all stages on the checklist (Appendix 3). Release is signed by the Development Manager, Quality Manager and Production Manager. The finished product specification (Appendix 4) is approved and operators are trained.

## 7. Changes to existing products
1. Every change is raised through change management (QM-001) and assessed under this procedure to the extent appropriate (not always all gates).
2. Changes always requiring review: allergens, claim-bearing ingredient, supplier of a high-risk material, CCP parameters, primary packaging (migration, seal integrity), shelf life.

## 8. Deviations
| Deviation | Action | Decision by |
|---|---|---|
| Shelf-life test out of criterion before end of shelf life | shorten shelf life or change recipe, assess batches on the market | Quality Manager |
| New allergen introduced to a line without risk assessment | stop trials, PR-005 assessment, clean and verify | Quality Manager |
| Factory trial not to specification | analyse, adjust recipe or process, repeat | Development Manager |
| Product launched without release | stop, risk assessment, PR-013 if needed | Site Director |

## 9. Verification
At least annually the Quality Manager reviews shelf lives of products on sale (periodic test results, complaints). The internal audit covers at least one completed project.
